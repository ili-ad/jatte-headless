# Compliance Toolkit: Florida Construction Lien Law (Post-2023 Updates)

## Statutory Cross-Walk of Key Provisions

Below is a cross-walk of Florida’s updated construction lien law
provisions (effective after Oct. 1, 2023 via HB 331). Each item includes
an excerpt of the statute and bullet-point guidance on **Trigger
Events**, **Required Content/Formatting**, and
**Penalties/Consequences** for non-compliance.

### Notice of Commencement (NOC) – § 713.13, Fla. Stat.

> “A notice of commencement must be recorded and posted on the site of
> the improvement before the first inspection… If the direct contract
> amount is greater than $5,000, a certified copy of the recorded notice
> of commencement must be filed with the building permit authority
> before the first inspection (threshold increased from $2,500 to
> $5,000)[bilzin.com](https://www.bilzin.com/insights/publications/2023/06/florida-enacts-changes-to-construction-lien-law#:~:text=Chapter%20713,amount%20from%20%242%2C500%20to%20%245%2C000).
> The notice of commencement is effective for 1 year unless a longer
> period is stated, and **any payments made by the owner after the
> expiration of the notice of commencement are considered improper
> payments**[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=commencement%20expresses%20a%20period%20of,commencement%20are%20considered%20improper%20payments)[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).”

  - **Trigger Events:** Must be **recorded and posted** **prior to
    starting work / first inspection** on the
    project[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).
    (Building departments now require proof of a recorded NOC for
    projects \> $5,000 before the first
    inspection[bilzin.com](https://www.bilzin.com/insights/publications/2023/06/florida-enacts-changes-to-construction-lien-law#:~:text=Chapter%20713,amount%20from%20%242%2C500%20to%20%245%2C000).)
    Typically signed by the owner (only the owner can sign) and recorded
    up to 90 days before commencement. If work isn’t started within 90
    days of recording, the NOC becomes
    void[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,and%20of%20no%20further%20effect).
    An Amended NOC may be recorded if project scope or time is extended
    (to maintain effectiveness beyond 1 year).

  - **Required Content & Formatting:** Must be in **substantially the
    statutory
    form**[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,in%20substantially%20the%20following%20form)
    including: property description, general description of improvement,
    owner’s name and interest, contractor’s name, lender (if any),
    surety (if any, attach bond copy), persons designated to receive
    notices, and an **expiration date** (defaults to 1 year if not
    stated)[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=8,13%281%29%28b%29%2C%20Florida%20Statutes).
    It includes a boldfaced **“WARNING TO OWNER”** advising that failure
    to comply can result in paying twice for the
    project[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-13/#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).
    The NOC must be **signed and notarized by the
    owner**[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,notice%20in%20the%20clerk%E2%80%99s%20office),
    and a copy must be posted at the job site. If a payment bond exists,
    a copy of the bond **must be attached** to the recorded NOC
    (otherwise the bond protections are
    lost)[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,limits%20for%20serving%20any%20required).

  - **Penalties/Consequences:** Failing to record a required NOC (when
    project \> $5,000) or not posting it can lead to **loss of lien
    priority** for the project – liens from subcontractors/suppliers may
    relate back to an earlier date, increasing owner
    risk[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,notice%20of%20any%20of%20them).
    **Payments made after the NOC’s expiration are “improper payments”**
    that do *not* cut off lien
    rights[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=commencement%20expresses%20a%20period%20of,commencement%20are%20considered%20improper%20payments)[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT)
    – the owner can be forced to pay twice. An owner who doesn’t obtain
    a NOC when required will receive a statutory warning on the building
    permit (14-point all-caps) that failure to record may result in
    double
    payment[deltonafl.gov](https://deltonafl.gov/183/Florida-Lien-Law-for-Homeowners#:~:text=,%E2%80%9D).
    Also, if a required NOC isn’t in place, lienors can rely on the
    building permit info for notice
    requirements[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,c).
    In short, **not having a valid NOC or letting it lapse can destroy
    the “proper payment” defense**, meaning the owner cannot assert that
    payments were properly disbursed to avoid
    liens[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=commencement%20expresses%20a%20period%20of,commencement%20are%20considered%20improper%20payments).
    In extreme cases, knowingly false NOCs or misrepresentations could
    be considered fraudulent (e.g. false NOC termination, see
    §713.132(4)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,right%20of%20action%20for%20damages)).

### Notice to Owner (Preliminary Notice) – § 713.06(2)(a)–(c)

> “All lienors except laborers, as a prerequisite to perfecting a lien,
> **must serve a notice on the owner** setting forth the lienor’s name
> and address, a description of the property, and the nature of the
> services or materials furnished or to be furnished… The notice must be
> served **before commencing, or not later than 45 days after
> commencing**, to furnish labor, services, or materials, and **in any
> event before the owner’s final payment** to the contractor after the
> contractor’s final
> affidavit[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=shall%20serve%20the%20notice%20to,over%20other%20lienors%20in%20the).
> **Failure to serve the notice, or to timely serve it, is a complete
> defense to enforcement of a lien** by that
> person[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=commencing%2C%20or%20not%20later%20than,The%20serving%20of%20the).
> The notice **may be in substantially the following form** and must
> include the \[statutory\] warning…: ‘WARNING\! FLORIDA’S CONSTRUCTION
> LIEN LAW ALLOWS SOME UNPAID CONTRACTORS, SUBCONTRACTORS, AND MATERIAL
> SUPPLIERS TO FILE
> LIENS…’[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=WARNING%21%20FLORIDA%E2%80%99S%20CONSTRUCTION%20LIEN%20LAW,HAVE%20MADE%20PAYMENT%20IN%20FULL).”

  - **Trigger Events:** **Any subcontractor, sub-subcontractor, or
    material supplier not in direct contract with the owner** must serve
    a **Notice to Owner (NTO)** *as a condition to preserve lien
    rights*. The deadline is **within 45 days of first furnishing**
    labor or materials to the project (it can be served before starting,
    but not later than 45 days after
    starting)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=shall%20serve%20the%20notice%20to,over%20other%20lienors%20in%20the).
    **Importantly, the NTO must be served before the owner disburses
    final payment to the
    contractor**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=commencing%2C%20or%20not%20later%20than,over%20other%20lienors%20in%20the).
    Copies must also be sent to others: a sub-subcontractor or material
    supplier to a sub must send a copy of the NTO to the contractor (and
    to the sub, if a materialman to
    sub-sub)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=sufficient%20for%20identification%20of%20the,notice%20must%20be%20served%20before).
    If the NOC designates a secondary address for notices, send a copy
    there
    too[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,invalidate%20an%20otherwise%20valid%20lien)
    (failure to copy the designated recipient does *not* invalidate the
    lien, but it’s good
    practice)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,invalidate%20an%20otherwise%20valid%20lien)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=in%20addition%20to%20himself%20or,invalidate%20an%20otherwise%20valid%20lien).

  - **Required Content & Formatting:** The NTO must be **in writing**
    and **served according to §713.18 (by certified/actual delivery or
    posting)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/billsummaries/2023/html/3198#:~:text=The%20bill%20provides%20that%20documents,Law%20must%20be%20served%20by)**.
    It should **substantially follow the statutory
    form**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,contained%20in%20the%20following%20form).
    Key contents include: the lienor’s name and address, a description
    of the property, the general description of the services or
    materials provided, and the person who ordered the work (e.g. the
    hiring
    contractor)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=NOTICE%20TO%20OWNER).
    The **statutory form includes prominent warnings to the owner** in
    bold type, such as: **“WARNING… unpaid contractors… can file liens…
    EVEN IF YOU HAVE PAID YOUR
    CONTRACTOR”**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=WARNING%21%20FLORIDA%E2%80%99S%20CONSTRUCTION%20LIEN%20LAW,HAVE%20MADE%20PAYMENT%20IN%20FULL)
    and advice that to avoid liens the owner should obtain releases
    whenever paying the
    contractor[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=UNDER%20FLORIDA%20LAW%2C%20YOUR%20FAILURE,PROPERTY%20AND%20YOUR%20PAYING%20TWICE).
    It also provides “Important Information for Your Protection,” urging
    the owner to learn about the lien law and consider consulting an
    attorney or the
    DBPR[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=%E2%80%94RECOGNIZE%20that%20this%20Notice%20to,to%20Owner%20have%20been%20paid).
    The notice must list the lienor’s name, address, and signature, and
    copies should be sent to all parties required by statute (owner,
    contractor if applicable, and anyone designated in the
    NOC)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=Copies%20to%3A%20%C2%A0%C2%A0,06%282%29%28a%29%20and%20%28b%29%2C%20Florida%20Statutes).
    *(The form may be combined with a Notice to Contractor on bonded
    jobs, titled “NOTICE TO OWNER/NOTICE TO
    CONTRACTOR”[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=Statutes).)*

  - **Penalties/Consequences:** **Failing to serve the NTO on time is an
    absolute bar to a lien** – it is a **complete defense** for the
    owner if a lienor who was required to give notice did not do so
    within 45
    days[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=commencing%2C%20or%20not%20later%20than,The%20serving%20of%20the).
    There is no excuse for missing the deadline; even good-faith
    mistakes in timing result in loss of lien rights (the law strictly
    requires compliance with
    timing)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,a).
    Content errors that don’t mislead (e.g. minor mistakes in the
    property description) may be forgiven if no one is adversely
    affected[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,a),
    but omission of the **mandatory warning language** or failing to
    include key information could jeopardize the notice’s validity.
    Moreover, an owner has **no obligation to pay lienors who failed to
    serve an NTO**: even if listed in a contractor’s affidavit, if their
    45-day notice window lapsed, the owner can exclude them from
    payment[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,c%291).
    **Bottom line:** Subs/suppliers who do not timely serve the Notice
    to Owner forfeit lien rights, and owners can assert improper notice
    as a defense to paying twice. (Owners should keep copies of all NTOs
    received; a lender who is served with an NTO must disburse funds
    consistent with proper payment rules or else be liable to the owner
    for
    damages[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=renders%20the%20lender%20liable%20to,proper%20payments%20under%20this%20paragraph)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,a).)

### Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit – § 713.06(3)(d)

> “When the final payment under a direct contract becomes due, the
> contractor **shall give to the owner a final payment affidavit**
> stating, if that be the fact, that all lienors under his or her direct
> contract who have timely served a notice to owner have been paid in
> full or, if not, listing the name of each lienor not paid and the
> amount
> due[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,contract%20becomes%20due%20the%20contractor)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=1,in%20substantially%20the%20following%20form)…
> The contractor shall have no lien or right of action against the owner
> for labor, services, or materials while in default of not giving the
> affidavit; however, a negligent inclusion or omission that has not
> prejudiced the owner does not defeat an otherwise valid lien. The
> contractor **must execute and deliver the affidavit to the owner at
> least 5 days before filing an action to enforce the
> lien**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=The%20contractor%20shall%20have%20no,terminated%20for%20a%20reason%20other)…
> **If the owner pays without obtaining the affidavit, the property
> shall be subject to the full amount of all valid liens of which the
> owner has notice** at the time the contractor furnished the
> affidavit[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=6,furnishes%20his%20or%20her%20affidavit).”

  - **Trigger Events:** **Final payment**: Before the owner makes the
    **final payment to the contractor**, and as a **condition precedent
    to the contractor enforcing any lien rights**, the contractor **must
    furnish a Final Payment Affidavit** to the
    owner[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,contract%20becomes%20due%20the%20contractor).
    This affidavit is typically given *when the contractor requests
    final payment* (or when the project is terminated early before
    completion)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=5,d%291).
    Even if the final payment isn’t yet due (e.g. project not finished
    due to termination), the contractor must give this affidavit at
    least 5 days **before filing any lien foreclosure
    lawsuit**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=The%20contractor%20shall%20have%20no,terminated%20for%20a%20reason%20other).
    (Best practice is to always provide it upon project completion or
    termination, well before any litigation, to allow the owner to see
    any outstanding bills.)

  - **Required Content & Formatting:** The affidavit must be a **sworn
    statement listing all unpaid lienors (subs/suppliers) who have
    served NTOs** and remain unpaid, with the amounts due to
    each[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=1,in%20substantially%20the%20following%20form)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,except%20the%20following%20listed%20lienors).
    If everyone is paid in full, the affidavit states that. Florida’s
    statute provides a **template form titled “Contractor’s Final
    Payment Affidavit”** including: the contractor’s information, a
    reference to the direct contract, a statement that all work is
    complete, and a table to list any unpaid lienors and
    amounts[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=CONTRACTOR%E2%80%99S%20FINAL%20PAYMENT%20AFFIDAVIT)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,except%20the%20following%20listed%20lienors).
    It must be **signed and notarized** by the contractor or an officer
    of the contractor’s
    company[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=Before%20me%2C%20the%20undersigned%20authority%2C,her%20personal%20knowledge%20the%20following)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=).
    (Interim/Progress Payment Affidavits: While not explicitly required
    by statute, **owners may contractually require a contractor’s
    affidavit with each progress payment** listing who has been paid to
    date[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=except%20the%20final%20payment%3A).
    In such cases, the content is similar – listing any unpaid bills up
    to that draw. These interim affidavits, or partial lien waivers, are
    a best practice to keep everyone informed and ensure proper use of
    funds.)

  - **Penalties/Consequences:** A contractor who **fails to give the
    final affidavit has no lien rights or right to sue** the owner for
    payment until it is
    provided[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=The%20contractor%20shall%20have%20no,terminated%20for%20a%20reason%20other).
    In fact, the contractor is in default under the lien law if they
    don’t furnish the affidavit, **blocking any lien enforcement**
    action until cured. If the affidavit is never given, the
    contractor’s lien claim can be defeated in court. Additionally,
    **if an owner pays the final balance to the contractor without first
    obtaining the affidavit**, the law **subjects the property to the
    liens of any subs who are owed money and gave proper notice** (even
    if the owner already paid the
    contractor)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=6,furnishes%20his%20or%20her%20affidavit).
    In other words, the owner loses the protection against undisclosed
    liens – an owner who pays without the affidavit essentially “pays at
    their own risk.” Conversely, if the owner *does* obtain the
    affidavit, the owner **has the right to rely on it** when disbursing
    final
    payment[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,be%20due%20to%20the%20contractor).
    If lienors are later found who were not listed but had given notice,
    the owner should pay them (and can deduct or withhold that from the
    contractor)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,be%20due%20to%20the%20contractor).
    The law also allows the owner, upon receiving an affidavit that
    lists unpaid bills, to **withhold final funds and directly pay those
    listed subcontractors/suppliers** after giving the contractor a
    10-day
    notice[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=2,or%20by%20any%20other%20person).
    Ultimately, non-compliance by the contractor (e.g. not providing the
    affidavit or providing a false affidavit) can lead to loss of lien
    rights. **Minor errors or omissions in the affidavit will not
    invalidate the contractor’s lien if the owner wasn’t
    prejudiced**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=The%20contractor%20shall%20have%20no,terminated%20for%20a%20reason%20other),
    but any willful false statements could expose the contractor to
    fraud allegations.

### Notice of Termination of Notice of Commencement – § 713.132

> “An owner may terminate the period of effectiveness of a notice of
> commencement by executing, swearing to, and recording a **Notice of
> Termination** that contains: (a) the same information as the notice of
> commencement; (b) the recording office’s reference number and
> recording date of the NOC; (c) a termination date (not earlier than 30
> days after recording the notice); (d) whether it applies to all or
> part of the property; (e) a statement that **all lienors have been
> paid in full**; and (f) a statement that the owner, **before
> recording**, has served a copy of this notice on each lienor who has a
> direct contract with the owner and on each lienor who timely served a
> notice to owner, and that the owner will serve a copy on any lienor
> who timely serves a notice to owner after recording. (No service is
> required to lienors who executed a final lien
> waiver)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,have%20been%20paid%20in%20full)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,in%20the%20notice%20of%20commencement).
> 
> The notice of termination **must be accompanied by the contractor’s
> final payment affidavit** (or a statement that no final affidavit is
> required)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,accompanied%20by%20the%20contractor%E2%80%99s%20affidavit).
> If properly served before recording, the notice of termination
> **terminates the notice of commencement** 30 days after recording (or
> on a later date stated in the
> notice)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,termination%20as%20the%20date%20on).
> However, if a lienor without direct contract began work before
> termination and later serves an NTO, the owner must serve that lienor
> with the notice of termination, and for that lienor the NOC will
> terminate 30 days after **service** of the notice of
> termination[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=which%20the%20notice%20of%20commencement,of%20the%20notice%20of%20termination).
> **If the owner or contractor fraudulently makes any false statement in
> the notice of termination or affidavit, they are liable to any lienor
> damaged by the fraudulent
> notice**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,right%20of%20action%20for%20damages).”

  - **Trigger Events:** An owner may **record a Notice of Termination**
    of the NOC **once the project is complete or all lienors have been
    paid** in full (or appropriately
    pro-rated)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=contractor%E2%80%99s%20affidavit).
    In practice, this is used to cut off the duration of a Notice of
    Commencement before its normal expiration. **All lienors must be
    paid (or properly provided for) before terminating** the
    NOC[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,have%20been%20paid%20in%20full).
    The owner can choose to terminate the NOC when the job is finished
    and all bills paid, rather than waiting for the NOC’s 1-year default
    expiration. This often happens at project close-out or if the
    project is abandoned or terminated early.

  - **Required Content & Formatting:** The Notice of Termination must be
    **signed and sworn by the owner** and must repeat all the key
    details from the original NOC (property description, parties,
    etc.)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,contains%20all%20of%20the%20following).
    It must reference the original NOC’s recording information
    (book/page or instrument \# and
    date)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,in%20the%20notice%20of%20commencement).
    Crucially, it must state an **effective termination date** that is
    **at least 30 days after the notice is recorded** (you cannot
    terminate a NOC immediately; a minimum 30-day cushion is
    required)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,notice%20of%20termination%20is%20recorded).
    The notice must specify whether it covers the entire property or
    just a portion thereof (for partial
    terminations)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,property%20to%20which%20it%20applies).
    It must declare that **all lienors have been paid in full** (or by
    prorated distribution under
    §713.06(4))[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,have%20been%20paid%20in%20full).
    It also must state that **the owner has served a copy of this Notice
    of Termination on every lienor** who has a direct contract or who
    timely served an NTO **before** recording the notice, and that the
    owner will likewise serve any lienor who serves a late NTO after
    recording[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,20).
    (Service of the notice should be done by the same methods under
    §713.18 – e.g. certified mail, etc.) The **owner must attach or
    include the general contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit** (if there
    was a contractor) to the Notice of
    Termination[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,accompanied%20by%20the%20contractor%E2%80%99s%20affidavit),
    since this affidavit shows all lienors are paid or listed for
    payment – effectively, the final affidavit is required to legitimize
    the termination. The Notice of Termination is recorded in the county
    records just like the NOC.

  - **Penalties/Consequences:** If the owner fails to **serve copies on
    all required lienors before recording** the notice, the attempted
    termination is **not effective against those lienors**. Only a
    properly served notice will cut off the NOC after 30
    days[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,termination%20as%20the%20date%20on).
    Lienors who were not served can claim that the NOC remained in
    effect for them (giving them the full lien period). Moreover, if a
    subcontractor started work before the termination and later (within
    45 days) serves an NTO after the Notice of Termination was recorded,
    the owner must serve that sub with the Notice of Termination at that
    time – and for that sub, the NOC termination date is 30 days after
    *service* to
    them[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=which%20the%20notice%20of%20commencement,of%20the%20notice%20of%20termination).
    **Failing to serve that sub will keep the NOC effective for that
    sub’s lien rights** until normal expiration. In short, improper
    service can lead to the NOC still being considered active as to
    certain lienors, meaning they retain lien rights beyond the 30-day
    cutoff.

Furthermore, filing a Notice of Termination **before all lienors are
paid** or without the contractor’s affidavit is dangerous: the law
explicitly makes the owner (and contractor) liable for **fraudulent
notices** – if any false statement is made in the notice or accompanying
affidavit (e.g. saying “all lienors paid” when they were not), any
affected lienor can sue for
damages[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,right%20of%20action%20for%20damages).
This could also undermine the owner’s defense in a lien foreclosure
(courts will not honor a fraudulent termination). **Summary:** use the
Notice of Termination only when appropriate (all known lien claims are
satisfied) and follow the service steps meticulously. When done
correctly, it shortens the window during which new liens can be recorded
by ending the NOC’s effectiveness 30 days after recording (or later
stated
date)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,termination%20as%20the%20date%20on).

*(Note: Even after a Notice of Termination, a lienor who was properly
served with it still has those 30 days to record a claim of lien for any
unpaid work. Also, terminating the NOC early does not discharge
already-recorded liens; it simply cuts off new lien rights arising from
that NOC.)*

### Construction Defect Notice & Opportunity to Repair – Chapter 558, Fla. Stat.

> “In actions alleging a construction defect, the claimant shall, **at
> least 60 days before filing any action** (120 days for an association
> of 20+ parcels), **serve written notice of claim** on the contractor,
> subcontractor, supplier, or design professional, as applicable,
> **referring to Chapter
> 558**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,with%20whom%20the%20claimant%20contracted).
> The notice **must describe in reasonable detail** the nature of each
> alleged construction defect and, if known, the damage or loss
> resulting from the defect, and **must identify the location** of each
> defect to enable the party to locate
> it[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,for%20purposes%20of%20this%20notice).
> The claimant should endeavor to serve this notice within 15 days of
> discovering the
> defect[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,8).
> 
> Upon service of the claim notice, the contractor (or other party) has
> **30 days (or 50 for large associations) to inspect** the property and
> *may* forward the notice to any subcontractors or others
> responsible[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=subcontractor%2C%20or%20other%20party%20that,be%20responsible%20for%20the%20defects).
> Within **45 days after receiving the notice**, the contractor (or
> party) **must serve a written response** to the claimant, choosing one
> of the statutory options: offer to repair the defect at no cost, offer
> to pay money, offer a combination of repair and money, **or** a
> statement disputing the claim and refusing to remedy
> it[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=How%20does%20a%20contractor%20respond,an%20appropriate%20reply%20may%20be)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,the%20completion%20of%20such%20repairs).
> If they offer to settle or repair, a detailed plan and timetable must
> be
> included[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,the%20completion%20of%20such%20repairs).
> If the contractor disputes the claim or fails to respond within the
> 45-day window, the owner may proceed to file a lawsuit after the
> 60-day pre-suit period
> expires[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,unresolved%20portions%20of%20the%20claim).
> A claimant who receives a settlement offer must accept or reject it in
> writing within 45 days, or else a later suit can be stayed until that
> step is
> done[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,claimant%20complies%20with%20this%20subsection).”

  - **Trigger Events:** **Prior to any lawsuit for construction
    defects** in Florida, Chapter 558 requires a pre-suit notice
    process. The **property owner (or association)** who claims defects
    must **serve a written Notice of Claim on the responsible parties at
    least 60 days before filing suit** (for a single home or small
    project; **120 days before suit for HOAs or condos with \>20
    units**)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,with%20whom%20the%20claimant%20contracted).
    This notice is typically triggered when significant construction
    deficiencies are discovered. (This is a **reactive** process – it
    comes into play if post-construction issues arise that could lead to
    litigation. It’s included in this toolkit so that contractors and
    platforms remain aware of the defect dispute resolution procedure as
    part of the project close-out and warranty phase.)

  - **Required Content & Procedure:** The Chapter 558 notice must be a
    **written document** (usually a letter) **identifying the
    construction defects in reasonable
    detail**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,for%20purposes%20of%20this%20notice).
    It should specify the nature of each defect, the location (e.g.
    “north elevation window leaks” or “foundation cracking at Unit
    4”), and any resulting damage known at that time. It should
    reference that it’s being sent pursuant to Chapter 558 (to invoke
    the statute). The notice should be delivered by certified mail or a
    delivery method that provides proof, and it is often sent to the
    last known address of the contractor, design professional, or other
    applicable party. **After the notice is served:** the recipient has
    a right to inspect the property within the next 30 days (15 days for
    the recipient to notify and pass it to their subcontractors who
    might be responsible, plus an additional 15 for the sub’s
    inspection, in some
    cases)[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=subcontractor%2C%20or%20other%20party%20that,be%20responsible%20for%20the%20defects).
    The recipient should coordinate access to examine the alleged
    defects. By **45 days after receiving the notice**, the recipient
    must **send a written response** to the
    claimant[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,The%20written%20response%20must%20provide).
    The response must choose one of the statutory options:
    
      - offer to **repair** the defect completely at no cost (with a
        description of the fix and
        schedule)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,the%20completion%20of%20such%20repairs);
    
      - offer to **pay money** for the defect (settlement), with or
        without involving
        insurance[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=defect%2C%20and%20a%20timetable%20for,the%20completion%20of%20such%20repairs);
    
      - offer a **combined repair and payment** (e.g. partial fix and
        partial monetary
        compensation)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,such%20repairs%20and%20making%20payment);
    
      - **deny** the claim (state that the contractor disputes the
        defect and will not remedy or
        pay)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=completion%20of%20such%20repairs%20and,making%20payment);
        or
    
      - in some cases, state that **insurance will determine coverage**
        (with an interim response within 30 days by the
        insurer)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,and%20settle%20the%20claim%3B%20or).  
        This response must be served on the claimant (typically by
        certified mail or delivery). If an offer is made, the owner has
        45 days to accept or reject it in
        writing[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,claimant%20complies%20with%20this%20subsection).
        During this pre-suit period, the statutes essentially pause any
        litigation – if the owner tries to sue too early, the court will
        stay the case until the 558 process is
        completed[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,claimant%20complies%20with%20this%20subsection).

  - **Penalties/Consequences:** Chapter 558 is essentially a **mandatory
    step** before litigation. **Non-compliance can result in a case
    being stayed or dismissed.** If an owner files a lawsuit without
    first serving the 558 notice and waiting the required time, the
    defendant can move to **stay the lawsuit** until the notice and
    response period are
    exhausted[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,claimant%20complies%20with%20this%20subsection).
    Thus, owners have strong incentive to comply (or risk delaying their
    day in court). From the contractor’s perspective, **failing to
    respond** to a 558 notice within the 45 days allows the owner to
    proceed straight to litigation after the 60-day
    period[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,unresolved%20portions%20of%20the%20claim).
    Not responding (or outright denying the claim) might also foreclose
    an opportunity to do a voluntary repair and mitigate damages. If the
    contractor offers to repair or pay and the owner unreasonably
    rejects the offer, that could potentially affect attorney’s fee
    recoveries later (the court will consider whether a party
    participated in the 558 process in good faith). While Chapter 558 is
    not directly about liens, it’s part of the **project close-out and
    dispute resolution workflow** – for instance, if an owner withholds
    final payment due to defects, the contractor’s ability to cure under
    558 may help avoid a lien or lawsuit. **Best practice:** treat a 558
    Notice seriously, document your response, involve your insurance
    carrier if appropriate, and always track the deadlines (30 days to
    inspect, 45 days to respond,
    etc.)[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=subcontractor%2C%20or%20other%20party%20that,be%20responsible%20for%20the%20defects)[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=How%20does%20a%20contractor%20respond,an%20appropriate%20reply%20may%20be).
    Properly integrating the 558 process in a project’s workflow
    (especially via any project management platform) can help resolve
    defects without court, or at least ensure compliance with statutory
    prerequisites.

## Notices & Affidavits Timeline – From Groundbreaking to Final Payment

To visualize how these notices and documents interact over a project’s
life cycle, below is a chronological **timeline/flowchart** of the key
steps, parties responsible, and required timing from pre-construction to
final payment (and beyond, if disputes arise):

1.  **Before Construction Begins:**
    
      - **Owner** (or Contractor on Owner’s behalf) **records a Notice
        of Commencement (NOC)** in the county records **and posts a copy
        at the job
        site**[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).
        This must be done **prior to the first inspection**. If the
        direct contract \> $5,000, a certified copy of the NOC must also
        be filed with the building permit authority before work
        starts[bilzin.com](https://www.bilzin.com/insights/publications/2023/06/florida-enacts-changes-to-construction-lien-law#:~:text=Chapter%20713,amount%20from%20%242%2C500%20to%20%245%2C000).
        The NOC designates official project information and stakeholders
        (owner, contractor, lender, etc.) and protects against liens if
        properly followed.
    
      - *(If applicable)* **General Contractor provides a Payment Bond
        and it’s attached to the
        NOC**[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,limits%20for%20serving%20any%20required).
        If a bond is in place and referenced, lien claims will be
        against the bond (notice requirements then also include Notice
        to Contractor under §713.23, not detailed here). If no bond,
        proceed with lien law steps.

2.  **Project Commencement & Ongoing Work:**
    
      - **Subcontractors, sub-subcontractors, and suppliers** who are
        not in direct contract with the owner begin furnishing
        labor/materials. **Within 45 days of starting work or delivering
        materials, each must serve a Notice to Owner (NTO)** on the
        Owner (and copy the Contractor) to preserve lien
        rights[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=shall%20serve%20the%20notice%20to,over%20other%20lienors%20in%20the)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=sufficient%20for%20identification%20of%20the,notice%20must%20be%20served%20before).
        Ideally, they serve it as early as possible (even before or
        right when they start) to avoid any deadline issues.
    
      - **Owner/Contractor** should collect and file all NTOs received.
        Owners may designate a secondary address in the NOC to receive
        NTO copies; any designated recipient should also get
        copies[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,invalidate%20an%20otherwise%20valid%20lien).
    
      - **During construction**, at each progress payment interval:
        
          - **Contractor** typically submits a pay application.
            **Owner** (or lender) should verify that all subs who served
            NTOs are accounted for. Under the lien law, **owner must
            make “proper payments” by ensuring that known lienors are
            paid** each
            draw[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=except%20the%20final%20payment%3A).
            The owner can (and often should) require **interim lien
            waivers or affidavits** from the contractor listing who has
            been paid and what amounts are
            due[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=except%20the%20final%20payment%3A).
        
          - **Contractor** provides **Partial (Progress) Releases of
            Lien** from itself and key subs/suppliers for the work
            covered by that payment. Florida law provides statutory
            **“Waiver and Release of Lien Upon Progress Payment”**
            forms that lienors use to waive lien rights to the extent of
            work paid to a certain
            date[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,in%20substantially%20the%20following%20form)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=).
            (These forms preserve any retainage or pending claims, and
            cannot be varied by
            contract[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=WAIVER%20AND%20RELEASE%20OF%20LIEN,UPON%20FINAL%20PAYMENT)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=By%3A).)
        
          - **Owner** disburses the progress payment. The owner is then
            protected from liens for the work covered by any waivers
            obtained. *(If the payment is insufficient to cover all
            lienors who gave notice, the owner must prorate and pay them
            proportionally[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=time%20of%20making%20a%20payment).)*
            Owners should **not pay the contractor for work unless lien
            waivers (or an affidavit) indicate that all sub-lienors for
            that work are being paid**, to maintain “proper payment”
            status[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=except%20the%20final%20payment%3A).
    
      - Throughout the project, if there are any changes such as a new
        lender or contractor, or an extension of the project beyond 1
        year, consider recording an **Amended NOC** to update that info
        (not statutorily mandated, but a best practice so that notices
        reach the right parties and the NOC doesn’t lapse).

3.  **Project Completion / Final Phase:**
    
      - **Contractor reaches substantial/final completion.** All or most
        work is done.
    
      - **Before final payment:**
        
          - **Contractor prepares a Final Payment Affidavit** listing
            any remaining unpaid lienors (who timely served NTOs) and
            the amounts
            due[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,contract%20becomes%20due%20the%20contractor)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,except%20the%20following%20listed%20lienors).
            This must be given to the Owner **at least 5 days before
            filing a lien foreclosure lawsuit**, but in practice it is
            given as a condition to receiving final
            payment[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=The%20contractor%20shall%20have%20no,terminated%20for%20a%20reason%20other).
        
          - **Owner (or Contractor)** obtains **Final Waivers of Lien**
            from the contractor and all subs/suppliers in exchange for
            final checks. Florida has a statutory **“Waiver and Release
            of Lien Upon Final Payment”** form to release lien rights
            upon receipt of final
            payment[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=WAIVER%20AND%20RELEASE%20OF%20LIEN,UPON%20FINAL%20PAYMENT)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=DATED%20on%20%C2%A0%2C%20%C2%A0%C2%A0%28year%29%C2%A0%C2%A0).
        
          - **Owner reviews Contractor’s Final Affidavit:**
            
              - If the affidavit states everyone is paid (no claims
                listed), the owner can proceed to final payment *in
                reliance on that
                affidavit*[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,be%20due%20to%20the%20contractor).
                The owner should ensure no new NTOs have been received
                that aren’t accounted for.
            
              - If the affidavit lists any unpaid lienors, the owner
                **must ensure those are paid** (from the final payment
                funds) before or at final payment to contractor. The
                owner can either write joint checks, directly pay those
                subs, or require payment and lien waivers, per
                §713.06(3)(d)2[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=2,or%20by%20any%20other%20person).
                The owner may withhold final funds sufficient to cover
                listed claims and any other lienors who have given
                notice but aren’t
                listed[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=3,4).
    
      - **Owner makes Final Payment** to contractor (after all
        conditions above are satisfied). **Owner retains proof** of all
        releases and the contractor’s affidavit. **At this point, if all
        went correctly, no potential lienors remain unpaid.**

4.  **Post-Construction:**
    
      - **Owner (optional): Record a Notice of Termination of NOC.** If
        the NOC’s effective period still has time remaining but the job
        is finished and everyone paid, the owner may formally terminate
        the NOC by recording a Notice of
        Termination[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=contractor%E2%80%99s%20affidavit).
        *Prerequisites:* Contractor’s final affidavit must accompany it;
        all lienors paid; serve notice of termination on all who gave
        NTO (and any who give NTO after, as
        described)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,20)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,termination%20as%20the%20date%20on).
        The NOC will cease to be effective 30 days after recording
        (cutting off the window for any new liens under that
        NOC)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,termination%20as%20the%20date%20on).
        If any lienor is inadvertently omitted from service, their lien
        rights might still exist (so be thorough).
    
      - **Any Claim of Lien filings:** Ideally, if proper payments were
        made and all releases obtained, no one should file a Claim of
        Lien. However, if a subcontractor or supplier was **not paid**
        and has preserved lien rights, they may record a **Claim of
        Lien** in county records. This typically must be done **within
        90 days of that lienor’s last furnishing of labor/material**
        (this deadline isn’t in the question’s statutes but is §713.08).
        The lienor must also **serve a copy of the Claim of Lien on the
        owner** within 15 days of recording. A lien foreclosure lawsuit
        must be filed within 1 year of the claim of lien (unless
        shortened).
    
      - **Owner’s response to a Claim of Lien:** If a lien is recorded,
        the owner (or contractor/general if dealing with subs) can take
        reactive steps. For instance:
        
          - File a **Notice of Contest of Lien** in court to shorten the
            foreclosure deadline to 60 days (forcing the lienor to sue
            quickly or lose the lien).
        
          - Alternatively, **transfer the lien to a bond** by obtaining
            a lien transfer bond (note: HB 331 increased the required
            bond amount to lien amount + 3 years interest + $5,000 or
            25%, up from $1,000 or
            25%)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/billsummaries/2023/html/3198#:~:text=,lien%20and%20a%20certificate%20of)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/billsummaries/2023/html/3198#:~:text=clerk%E2%80%99s%20office%20to%20transfer%20a,of%20the%20recorded%20notice%20on).
        
          - Engage in settlement negotiations or withhold payment if the
            lien is due to a subcontractor not paid by the contractor
            (the owner may use the contractor’s breach as a defense or
            counterclaim).
        
          - If the lien is invalid or exaggerated, seek its discharge
            through court (and possibly recover attorney’s fees as
            prevailing
            party[bilzin.com](https://www.bilzin.com/insights/publications/2023/06/florida-enacts-changes-to-construction-lien-law#:~:text=Chapter%20713,have%20been%20transferred%20to%20security)).
    
      - **Construction Defect claims (Chapter 558):** After project
        completion (or even during warranty period), if the owner
        discovers any construction defects, they must follow the
        **Chapter 558 notice process** *before* filing any lawsuit or
        arbitration for
        defects[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,with%20whom%20the%20claimant%20contracted).
        
          - The owner serves a **558 Notice of Claim** describing
            defects.
        
          - The contractor (and/or subs) have an opportunity to inspect
            and must respond with an offer to fix or other proposal
            within 45
            days[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=How%20does%20a%20contractor%20respond,an%20appropriate%20reply%20may%20be).
        
          - This process adds roughly 60 days (single home) or 120 days
            (for larger associations) to the timeline before any
            litigation. During that time, the parties might resolve the
            issue by repairs or settlement.
        
          - If unresolved, after the notice period the owner can proceed
            with a construction defect lawsuit. (The Chapter 558 process
            does not directly affect lien rights, but often payment
            disputes and defect disputes intertwine – e.g. an owner
            might withhold final payment due to defects, triggering both
            lien claims by the contractor and defect claims by the
            owner. Both processes must be navigated in parallel.)
    
      - **Warranty and Close-out:** Contractors may provide a separate
        warranty; any callbacks for repairs typically occur without
        litigation. If disputes are resolved amicably, no further legal
        notices are needed.

*(See the Compliance Checklist below for a one-page summary of key steps
and responsibilities.)*

## Compliance Checklist for Contractors & Platforms

This concise checklist distills the critical compliance steps under
Florida’s lien law. **Contractors, project owners, and construction
management software platforms** can use this as a workflow guide to
ensure no statutory requirement is missed. (All references to days are
calendar days unless otherwise noted.)

  - **Before Work Begins:**
    
      - **Record Notice of Commencement (NOC)** (Owner to record & sign,
        before first
        inspection)[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).
        Attach any payment bond to the
        NOC[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,limits%20for%20serving%20any%20required).
        Post a copy of NOC at job site.
    
      - **File NOC with Permit** (If contract \> $5,000, file certified
        copy with building department before
        starting)[bilzin.com](https://www.bilzin.com/insights/publications/2023/06/florida-enacts-changes-to-construction-lien-law#:~:text=Chapter%20713,amount%20from%20%242%2C500%20to%20%245%2C000).
    
      - **Obtain multiple copies** of the recorded NOC to distribute to
        contractor and major subs/suppliers.

  - **When Subcontractors/Suppliers Begin Work:**
    
      - **Collect Notices to Owner (NTO)** from all subs, sub-subs, and
        suppliers **within 45 days of their
        start**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=shall%20serve%20the%20notice%20to,over%20other%20lienors%20in%20the).
        (Subcontractors/Suppliers: ensure you serve the Owner (and copy
        the Contractor) your NTO by certified mail or delivery).
    
      - **Log all NTOs** in a tracking system (date received, sending
        party). If an NTO is misaddressed or info is wrong, flag it, but
        remember even with minor errors an NTO may still be enforceable
        if not
        prejudicial[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,a).
    
      - **If no NOC was recorded** (e.g., for very small projects), subs
        can rely on building permit
        info[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,c)
        – but owners should almost always have an NOC for protection.

  - **During Construction (Each Payment Cycle):**
    
      - **Interim lien waivers**: Require **Partial Lien Waivers** from
        contractor and subs up to amount paid through each draw (use
        statutory “Progress Payment” waiver
        form)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,in%20substantially%20the%20following%20form).
    
      - **Contractor’s interim affidavit (if required)**: Owner may
        request a partial payment affidavit listing any unpaid bills
        before issuing a progress
        payment[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=except%20the%20final%20payment%3A).
        (Contractors: provide this on request; platforms can automate an
        affidavit form each pay period.)
    
      - **Verify Notices to Owner**: Before releasing a payment, ensure
        you have NTOs on file for all subs/material suppliers on that
        pay application. **Do not pay for work from an NTO-sub until you
        have their waiver for prior payments** (or will include them in
        joint
        check)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=UNDER%20FLORIDA%20LAW%2C%20YOUR%20FAILURE,PROPERTY%20AND%20YOUR%20PAYING%20TWICE).
    
      - **Proper payment distribution**: For each draw, Owner (or
        construction loan admin) must **pay all lienors who gave NTO**
        to avoid lien
        exposure[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=except%20the%20final%20payment%3A).
        If draw funds are limited, **prorate payment among NTO lienors**
        as
        required[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=time%20of%20making%20a%20payment)
        and document it. Never ignore an NTO and pay the contractor in
        full without addressing that sub – that would be an improper
        payment as to that lienor.
    
      - **Retain receipts** of all sent and received notices (NTOs,
        etc.), and proof of service (certified mail slips, etc.) in the
        project file.

  - **Close-Out (Project Completion):**
    
      - **Prepare Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit** (Contractor to
        Owner) listing all unpaid lienors (with NTOs) and amounts
        due[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,contract%20becomes%20due%20the%20contractor).
        Provide this to Owner *before final payment is made*.
        (Contractor must also swear to this at least 5 days before
        filing any lien
        lawsuit)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=The%20contractor%20shall%20have%20no,terminated%20for%20a%20reason%20other).
    
      - **Collect Final Lien Waivers** from all subs/suppliers for whom
        final payment is being made (use statutory final payment waiver
        form)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=WAIVER%20AND%20RELEASE%20OF%20LIEN,UPON%20FINAL%20PAYMENT).
        Also obtain a Final Release from the Contractor upon paying them
        in full.
    
      - **Owner’s final payment checklist**:
        
          - Confirm all subs/suppliers with NTO have either been paid in
            full or are listed on the Contractor’s affidavit with an
            amount due.
        
          - If any lienors are listed as unpaid on the affidavit:
            **withhold sufficient funds** from the contractor’s payment
            to pay those
            lienors[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=2,or%20by%20any%20other%20person).
            Either pay them directly (after giving contractor notice and
            10 days to
            pay)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=2,or%20by%20any%20other%20person)
            or issue joint checks at closing.
        
          - Ensure no new NTOs have arrived late that weren’t accounted
            for (if so, include them in payment distribution).
        
          - Verify the final affidavit has been provided and is
            signed/notarized.
    
      - **Make Final Payment** to contractor *only after* the above
        conditions are
        met[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=5,been%20furnished%20to%20the%20owner).
        The owner should not release final funds without the final
        affidavit – by law the owner must **retain final payment until
        affidavit is
        received**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=5,been%20furnished%20to%20the%20owner).
    
      - **Record Notice of Termination** of NOC (Owner, optional but
        recommended if NOC still has long duration). Before recording:
        **serve all known lienors** (those with contract or NTO) with a
        copy[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,termination%20as%20the%20date%20on).
        Attach Contractor’s final affidavit. Specify a termination date
        ≥30 days
        out[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,notice%20of%20termination%20is%20recorded).
        Record in county records to officially close the NOC period.
    
      - **Secure all documentation**: Make sure the project file
        contains: recorded NOC, all NTOs and proofs of service, all
        interim and final lien waivers, Contractor’s Final Affidavit,
        Notice of Termination (if any), and any payment bond info. These
        will be vital if any disputes arise.

  - **If a Lienor (sub or supplier) goes unpaid and files a Claim of
    Lien:**
    
      - **Serve Notice of Contest of Lien** (optional, to shorten
        deadline) or be prepared for lien foreclosure timeline (1 year).
        Consult legal counsel promptly.
    
      - **Consider bonding off the lien** to remove it from property
        record (requires obtaining a surety bond or cash deposit per
        §713.24; note new 2023 bond amount
        increase)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/billsummaries/2023/html/3198#:~:text=the%20subject%20lien.%20,the%20lien%2C%20whichever%20is%20greater)[bilzin.com](https://www.bilzin.com/insights/publications/2023/06/florida-enacts-changes-to-construction-lien-law#:~:text=Chapter%20713,in%20the%20claim%20of%20lien%E2%80%A6%E2%80%9D).
    
      - **Avoid improper payments**: Do *not* pay the contractor if you
        know a sub has a valid lien – resolve the lien or ensure joint
        payment. Remember, **misapplication of construction funds is a
        crime** (contractors must use payments to pay subs/suppliers
        first)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,such%20services%2C%20labor%2C%20or%20materials)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,construction%20funds%2C%20punishable%20as%20follows).
    
      - **Communicate**: Often liens can be resolved by direct payment
        or negotiation. Obtain lien waivers upon any settlement payment.

  - **If construction defects are alleged by Owner (Chapter 558):**
    
      - **Owner sends 558 Notice of Claim** to contractor/design
        professional **at least 60 days before suit** (120 days for
        large
        HOAs)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,with%20whom%20the%20claimant%20contracted).
        Include detailed description of defects.
    
      - **Contractor** (upon receiving 558 notice): Acknowledge receipt,
        notify insurance carrier if needed, and within 30 days arrange
        inspection of the reported
        defects[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=subcontractor%2C%20or%20other%20party%20that,be%20responsible%20for%20the%20defects).
    
      - **Contractor’s 558 Response (due in 45 days):** Formulate a
        written offer or refusal as per statute (repair, monetary,
        combination, or
        denial)[floridahardhatlaw.com](https://www.floridahardhatlaw.com/faqs-about-chapter-558-under-floridas-construction-defects-statute/#:~:text=How%20does%20a%20contractor%20respond,an%20appropriate%20reply%20may%20be).
        Serve it on Owner timely.
    
      - **Owner**: evaluate any offer. If accepting, document the
        agreement and allow repair access. If rejecting or no response,
        after day 60/120, you may proceed to file suit.
    
      - **Document resolution**: If defects are repaired or settled,
        have a written release or repair confirmation to close the issue
        without litigation.

This checklist can be integrated into project management software to
trigger alerts (e.g., “Day 40 – check if NTO received from all subs;”
“Project 99% complete – request Final Affidavit from GC;” “Before
final payment – verify lien waivers”). By following these steps,
contractors and owners can significantly reduce lien-related risks and
ensure compliance with Florida law.

## Statutory Forms and Model Documents

Florida’s lien statutes include **template forms** for many of the
required notices and documents. Utilizing the statutory language
verbatim (or substantially similar) is highly recommended, as the law
sometimes mandates specific wording or font emphasis. Below are the key
forms and references:

  - **Notice of Commencement (Form)** – The required form is set forth
    in §713.13(1)(d). It includes spaces for all required information
    and the Owner’s notarized signature
    area[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=,in%20substantially%20the%20following%20form)[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-13/#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).
    Most county recorders and building departments provide fillable NOC
    forms (e.g., **County Recorder websites or DBPR**). **Example**: The
    statutory form in §713.13 includes the “WARNING TO OWNER” in
    14-point capitalized
    text[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-13/#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).
    Always use this format. *(For a downloadable template, see your
    local county’s official form – many counties like Broward, Polk,
    etc., have PDF NOC forms identical to the statute.)*

  - **Notice to Owner (Form)** – The statutory “Notice to Owner” form is
    provided in
    §713.06(2)(c)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,contained%20in%20the%20following%20form).
    It contains the bold warning heading and the “Important Information
    for Your Protection” text. **Model form**: begin with “To \[Owner’s
    Name\]…” and include the described services, property, and
    contractor info, followed by the warnings and the lienor’s
    signature[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=WARNING%21%20FLORIDA%E2%80%99S%20CONSTRUCTION%20LIEN%20LAW,HAVE%20MADE%20PAYMENT%20IN%20FULL)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=NOTICE%20TO%20OWNER).
    Using the exact wording (especially the warning paragraphs) is
    crucial. **Note:** This form can be combined with a Notice to
    Contractor (for bonded jobs) into one “Notice to
    Owner/Contractor”[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=Statutes)
    if needed.

  - **Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit (Form)** – The form is given
    in
    §713.06(3)(d)1[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=CONTRACTOR%E2%80%99S%20FINAL%20PAYMENT%20AFFIDAVIT)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,except%20the%20following%20listed%20lienors).
    It should be on the contractor’s letterhead or form and include: a
    sworn statement by the contractor listing all unpaid lienors and
    amounts due (or stating all have been paid). **Model form**: Titled
    “Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit,” with spaces for the
    contractor’s name, owner’s name, property description, and a list of
    unpaid lienors (name & amount) or a statement that all lienors have
    been paid, then signed and
    notarized[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=CONTRACTOR%E2%80%99S%20FINAL%20PAYMENT%20AFFIDAVIT)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=4,except%20the%20following%20listed%20lienors).
    (Many Florida contractors maintain a template of this affidavit to
    fill in project-specific info at close-out.)

  - **Interim/Progress Payment Affidavit** – Not a statutory form, but
    often a scaled-down version of the Final Payment Affidavit used for
    interim draws. It might state: “All lienors have been paid up
    through this payment, except the following…”. There’s no mandated
    text, but using similar language to the final affidavit (minus the
    5-day lawsuit clause) is wise. Some owners include a form in the
    contract.

  - **Waiver and Release of Lien Upon Progress Payment** – Statutory
    form at
    §713.20(4)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,in%20substantially%20the%20following%20form)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=).
    This is the **partial lien waiver** form. It specifies the amount
    and through-date of work/materials covered by the payment and leaves
    exceptions for retainage or later work. It must be **substantially
    in this format**, and no one can demand a different form per
    §713.20(6)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=WAIVER%20AND%20RELEASE%20OF%20LIEN,UPON%20FINAL%20PAYMENT)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=By%3A).
    **Obtain these waivers with each payment.**

  - **Waiver and Release of Lien Upon Final Payment** – Statutory form
    at
    §713.20(5)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=WAIVER%20AND%20RELEASE%20OF%20LIEN,UPON%20FINAL%20PAYMENT)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=).
    This is the **final lien waiver** stating that in consideration of
    final payment, the lienor waives and releases its lien rights for
    the project. Again, this form (or one substantially similar) must be
    used – a contractor cannot be forced to sign a non-statutory form
    that waives more rights than the statute
    allows[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=By%3A).
    Make sure the final payment amount is filled in and the lienor signs
    it upon receipt of the final check.

  - **Notice of Termination** – There is *no one official pre-filled
    form in the statute*, but §713.132 specifies all required
    elements[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,contains%20all%20of%20the%20following)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,have%20been%20paid%20in%20full).
    Many organizations (e.g., the Florida Department of Business &
    Professional Regulation or local Clerk of Court websites) provide a
    sample form. **Key fields**: project name, original NOC recording
    info, statement that all lienors are paid, termination date (30+
    days out), owner’s signature notarized, and a statement about
    service on lienors. Always attach the contractor’s final affidavit.
    A reference example can be found in the Florida Construction Lien
    Law forms provided by some county recorders or in Florida Bar
    publications.

  - **Notice of Contest of Lien / Notice of Contest of Bond** –
    Statutory forms in §§713.22 and 713.23. These are short forms the
    owner files with the court to start the 60-day clock on a lienor to
    sue. While not asked for in this toolkit, be aware they exist if you
    need to discharge a lingering lien.

  - **Chapter 558 Notice of Claim** – No statutory form given (since
    it’s just a letter), but **practical tip**: title your letter
    “Notice of Claim – Florida Statutes Chapter 558” and include the
    project, defect details, and recipient’s opportunity to inspect and
    respond. The **Florida Bar** or construction law firms often have
    templates for this pre-suit notice. The key is to include the info
    required by §558.004(1) (description of defects, location,
    etc.)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,for%20purposes%20of%20this%20notice)
    in a clear manner.

**Where to find these forms:** Official Florida Statutes (e.g., online
at statutes.leg.state.fl.us) publish the text of these forms. The
Florida Department of Business & Professional Regulation (DBPR) often
distributes a consumer **“Summary of the Construction Lien Law”**
pamphlet (per §713.135) which includes an explanation and sometimes
sample forms. Local Clerk of Court or Recorder offices provide
**fillable PDF forms** for NOC, Notice of Termination, and sometimes
Contractor’s Affidavits on their websites. Construction industry
services (SunRay, Levelset, etc.) also offer free templates that conform
to Florida law.

Always ensure the forms you use reflect the **latest statutory
language** (post-2023 changes). For example, HB 331 updated some forms
(NOC, Notice of Termination, and Notice of Nonpayment) – so use updated
versions. Using the statutory forms not only ensures compliance but also
speeds up approval by permitting authorities and reduces risk of
challenges.

## Common Pitfalls and Integration Challenges

Even with a roadmap, there are frequent mistakes and misunderstandings
that can derail lien rights or defenses. Below are some common
integration pitfalls and their consequences under current law:

  - **NOC Not Recorded or Expired:** If an owner fails to record a
    Notice of Commencement (or it expires during a long project and
    isn’t extended), payments made by the owner can become “improper
    payments.” This means an owner who paid the contractor might still
    have to pay subcontractors who file
    liens[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=commencement%20expresses%20a%20period%20of,commencement%20are%20considered%20improper%20payments)[m.flsenate.gov](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/713.13#:~:text=WARNING%20TO%20OWNER%3A%20ANY%20PAYMENTS,RECORDING%20YOUR%20NOTICE%20OF%20COMMENCEMENT).
    Without a valid NOC, subs aren’t required to serve an NTO (they can
    rely on the building permit
    info[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,c)),
    which can catch an owner by surprise with liens at the end. *Pitfall
    fix:* Always record the NOC and re-record if the project extends
    beyond the NOC’s stated expiration. If a NOC expires, **do not
    continue making payments** until it’s extended or a new NOC is
    recorded, otherwise you forfeit the statutory protection against
    double payment.

  - **Failure to Update NOC Info:** If the NOC’s property description is
    wrong or parties are misidentified, a lienor could be adversely
    affected. For instance, an incorrect legal description that misleads
    a lienor could render an owner’s payments improper as to that
    lienor[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,property%20where%20the%20improvements%20are).
    Also, if the ownership changes or contractor is replaced, not
    amending the NOC can lead to notices not reaching the correct party.
    *Best practice:* If any key detail (owner’s name, lender,
    contractor) changes, record an amended NOC. While the statute
    doesn’t explicitly require amendment, it helps ensure all lienors
    have correct information. Remember, a trivial clerical error in NOC
    is forgiven if it doesn’t mislead, but a substantial error that
    *does* harm a lienor can strip “proper payment”
    status[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=,property%20where%20the%20improvements%20are).

  - **Missed Notice to Owner (NTO):** Subcontractors or suppliers
    sometimes forget to send the Notice to Owner or send it late (after
    45 days). The consequence is severe: **no lien rights**
    whatsoever[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=commencing%2C%20or%20not%20later%20than,The%20serving%20of%20the).
    A judge will dismiss their lien claim as a matter of law. From the
    general contractor’s perspective, this can also harm relationships –
    the owner isn’t obligated to pay a sub who missed the notice,
    meaning the GC might have to cover it. *Solution:* **Platforms
    should auto-remind subs** of the NTO deadline as soon as they’re
    added to the job. Use certified mail logs or electronic delivery
    with confirmation (Florida allows certain e-delivery by agreement).
    Owners and GCs can also be proactive: if you hire a sub, and you
    don’t receive their NTO within, say, 30 days, send a friendly
    reminder – preserving their lien rights keeps everyone honest and
    the project funded.

  - **Improper Service of Notices:** All notices (NTO, NOC copies,
    termination notices, etc.) must be served by authorized methods
    (hand delivery, certified/registered mail, overnight with delivery
    confirmation, or posting when others
    fail)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/billsummaries/2023/html/3198#:~:text=The%20bill%20provides%20that%20documents,Law%20must%20be%20served%20by).
    Using regular email or standard mail (unless followed up properly)
    could be deemed insufficient. Not serving a required party (like not
    sending the NTO copy to the designated person on NOC, or an owner
    failing to serve all lienors a Notice of Termination) can undermine
    the notice. *Consequence:* The notice might be considered not given
    at all. For instance, a Notice of Termination not served on a known
    sub is ineffective as to that sub’s lien
    window[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,termination%20as%20the%20date%20on)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=which%20the%20notice%20of%20commencement,of%20the%20notice%20of%20termination).
    Always document service (certified mail receipts, etc.). If posting
    on site (last resort), take a photo of the posted notice with a
    timestamp.

  - **Ignoring the Contractor’s Final Affidavit:** Owners sometimes are
    unaware they should obtain this affidavit and thus make final
    payment without it. That is a **big mistake**. If a final affidavit
    wasn’t provided, the contractor is actually not entitled to enforce
    a
    lien[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=The%20contractor%20shall%20have%20no,terminated%20for%20a%20reason%20other),
    but if the owner pays in full and later a sub lien pops up, the
    owner loses the “rely on affidavit” defense. In fact, by statute if
    the owner doesn’t get the affidavit and still pays, **the property
    becomes subject to liens for any subs who haven’t been paid** (that
    the owner knew
    of)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/713.06#:~:text=6,furnishes%20his%20or%20her%20affidavit).
    *Mitigation:* Never issue final payment until the contractor
    delivers the affidavit. If the contractor refuses, the owner can
    withhold payment (and the contractor then cannot lien because
    they’re in default of providing the affidavit). This affidavit
    essentially locks in who can lien; without it, the owner is flying
    blind.

  - **Lien Waiver Pitfalls:** Using non-statutory lien waiver forms can
    be problematic. Florida law says you **cannot demand a lien waiver
    that’s broader than the statutory
    form**[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=By%3A).
    Sometimes contracts provide overly broad waiver forms (waiving all
    claims, etc.). Such waivers might be void or unenforceable in court.
    Also, not understanding that a conditional waiver (conditioned on
    the check clearing) is allowed – lienors can write that condition in
    if
    needed[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=lien%20that%20is%20different%20from,5).
    *Tip:* Stick to the statutory forms and add clarifying language
    about conditional payment if applicable. And ensure the through-date
    on partial waivers is correct (subs have been caught by signing
    waivers through the current date, which inadvertently waived lien
    rights for work performed *after* the payment period).

  - **Payment Mistakes & Misapplication of Funds:** By law, a contractor
    (or anyone receiving funds for construction) must use those funds to
    pay down the project’s bills
    first[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,such%20services%2C%20labor%2C%20or%20materials).
    **Using money from Project A to cover Project B** or failing to pay
    subs while taking payment can be deemed **misapplication of
    construction funds**. Under §713.345, if done **knowingly and
    intentionally**, it’s a crime – potentially a felony for larger
    amounts[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,construction%20funds%2C%20punishable%20as%20follows).
    Owners don’t want a contractor in that situation (work may stop,
    project could get liened by many). Contractors should have robust
    accounting to apply all owner payments to the job’s expenses.
    Owners, if paying via a title company or construction escrow, may
    require proof that previous funds were used to pay subs.
    *Significance:* Aside from criminal risk, misapplication often leads
    to liens (the funds didn’t reach the people who did the work). It
    also can result in license discipline for licensed
    contractors[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter713/All#:~:text=,129%281%29%28r).
    **Bottom line:** always use construction funds for their intended
    project and pay all “downstream” parties promptly. Owners, if you
    suspect misapplication (e.g., subs complain of non-payment while GC
    was paid), you might remind the contractor of §713.345 – it tends to
    get their attention.

  - **Not Responding to a 558 Notice (Defect claims):** If an owner
    serves a Chapter 558 notice and the contractor ignores it or delays
    beyond 45 days, the owner can directly sue, and the contractor loses
    the chance to fix the issue
    first[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2018/558.004#:~:text=,unresolved%20portions%20of%20the%20claim).
    This can also sour relations and end any hope of an easy resolution.
    Conversely, owners who don’t send the 558 notice and go straight to
    suing can have their case paused. *Integration:* Project management
    teams should have a task for any warranty/defect complaint:
    “Initiate 558 process.” Contractors should treat it formally – log
    the deadline, send a response by day 45, even if it’s a denial. Many
    claims get settled in this window if handled correctly, saving
    everyone time and money.

  - **Lack of Documentation:** In lien disputes, paper (or digital)
    trail is everything. If a contractor claims they sent an NTO or a
    final affidavit, but has no proof, it can lead to factual fights or
    loss of rights. If an owner says “I never got a Notice to Owner from
    that supplier,” but the supplier has a signed certified mail green
    card, the supplier will prevail on that issue. Not keeping copies of
    releases can mean an owner can’t prove a defense of payment.
    *Solution:* Implement a documentation protocol: every notice or
    waiver that comes in is scanned and stored in a dedicated project
    folder (and maybe cross-cataloged by date and type). Every outgoing
    notice – send it with a trackable method and save the proof
    (certified mail tracking, etc.). Use project management software to
    store these files where all stakeholders (owner, GC, perhaps lender)
    can access if needed. This not only prevents things from falling
    through cracks but also provides evidence if a lien foreclosure or
    other dispute occurs.

By anticipating these pitfalls, parties can adjust their processes. In
sum, **strict compliance** with notice deadlines, service methods, and
form requirements is the safest path – Florida courts enforce these lien
law requirements literally. At the same time, owners and contractors
should communicate: for example, if an owner hasn’t seen an NTO from a
known sub, ask the contractor (maybe it was mis-addressed). Everyone’s
goal should be a well-documented project where, at the end, the owner
pays once and all those who provided work are paid in full – following
the statute’s roadmap is the best way to get there.

resources, you’ll ensure your processes not only meet legal requirements
but also reflect **best practices** endorsed by experts in the field.

Finally, remember that Florida’s lien laws were significantly updated in
2023 – always verify that any guide or form you use is updated
**post-October 1, 2023**. The forward-looking approach (as we’ve taken
here) will keep your toolkit in line with current law, helping both in
**reactive situations** (like handling a lien or defect claim dispute)
and **proactive planning** (like setting up your project’s notice
schedule and documentation workflow from day one). Compliance may seem
complex, but with this roadmap and resources, you can navigate Florida’s
construction lien law with confidence and avoid the common traps that
lead to expensive problems.
