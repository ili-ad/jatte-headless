# Florida Lien Release Bonds (§ 713.24) – Case Law and Practical Guidance

## Overview of Lien Release (Discharge) Bonds in Florida

Florida’s Construction Lien Law allows property interests encumbered by
a mechanics’ lien to be **“bonded off”** – meaning the lien is
transferred from the real property to an alternate security (cash or
surety bond) under Florida Statutes
§ 713.24[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=an%20amount%20equal%20to%20the,7).
**Any interested party (typically the owner or general contractor)** may
post a **lien transfer bond** in the required amount (the lien amount
**plus** 3 years’ interest **plus** the greater of $1,000 or 25% of the
lien amount for
costs)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=an%20amount%20equal%20to%20the,7).
Once this bond (or cash deposit) is filed with the clerk, *“the real
property shall thereupon be released from the lien…and such lien shall
be transferred to said
security”*[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=permit%20any%20owner%2C%20whether%20or,the%20time%20it%20was%20transferred).
In practical terms, the property’s title is cleared and the lien
attaches to the bond instead, enabling owners to refinance or sell
despite the
dispute[paveselaw.com](https://paveselaw.com/five-options-for-responding-to-a-construction-lien/#:~:text=Since%20this%20course%20of%20action,who%20posted%20or%20deposited%20it).
The clerk of court records a certificate of transfer and must mail a
copy to the lienor, notifying them that the lien is now against the
bond[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=Once%20the%20transfer%20has%20taken,insufficient%20to%20pay%20the%20lienor%E2%80%99s).
From that point forward, the bond (and its surety) replaces the property
as the source of recovery for the
lienor[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=permit%20any%20owner%2C%20whether%20or,the%20time%20it%20was%20transferred).

**Key Effects:** The owner gains clear title free of the lien, and the
lienor gains a security (often backed by a surety company’s credit) in
place of the property. However, the lienor’s rights against that bond
are strictly governed by statute and Florida case law, as discussed
below. The bond’s **requirements and proper procedures** have been a
central issue in many Florida cases, which clarify how to correctly
execute and enforce a lien transfer bond.

## Strict Compliance with Deadlines for Enforcing Transferred Liens

Florida courts emphasize that **mechanics’ lien remedies are strictly
statutory** and must be enforced within definite time
frames[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Our%20supreme%20court%20has%20specified,%E2%80%9D%20Id)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time).
Under § 713.22, a lienor generally has one year from recording the lien
to file an action to enforce it (foreclose) unless this period is
**“otherwise shortened by operation of law.”** An owner (or other
interested party) can shorten the deadline by recording a **Notice of
Contest of Lien**, which triggers a **60-day deadline** for the lienor
to file
suit[paveselaw.com](https://paveselaw.com/five-options-for-responding-to-a-construction-lien/#:~:text=Option%202%3A%20%C2%A0The%20property%20owner,Claim%20of%20Lien%20is%20extinguished).
When a lien has been transferred to a bond, the Notice of Contest
similarly shortens the time to sue **“to enforce any claim of lien or
claim against a bond”** to 60
days[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=Section%20713,the%20lien%20is%20extinguished%20automatically)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=,%E2%80%9D).
Florida courts have consistently held that if the lienor fails to
**commence an action against the bond** within that 60-day window, *“the
lien…shall be extinguished automatically”* by operation of
law[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=to%20commence%20an%20action%20on,60%20days%20after%20service%20of)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084).
In such cases, the lien is null, and the clerk **must release the bond**
back to the principal or
surety[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084).

**Case Illustration – Hiller and Rabil:** In **Hiller v. Phoenix Assocs.
of S. Fla., Inc.**, a homeowner bonded off a contractor’s lien *after*
the contractor had filed a foreclosure lawsuit, and then served a notice
of contest of
lien[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=After%20Phoenix%20commenced%20the%20action,filed%20a%20motion%20for%20leave)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=Pursuant%20to%20the%20%E2%80%9Cunless%20otherwise,service%20of%20the%20notice%20of).
The contractor did not sue the surety within 60 days, instead attempting
to add the surety to the case after the deadline. The Second DCA
strictly enforced the statute: because the contractor *“failed to
commence an action on the surety within the shortened time period,”* the
lien transfer bond was deemed automatically extinguished and had to be
released[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=to%20commence%20an%20action%20on,60%20days%20after%20service%20of).
Similarly, in **Rabil v. Seaside Builders, LLC**, a contractor recorded
a lien and sued the owners, but the owners then transferred the lien to
a bond and filed a notice of
contest[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=and%20filed%20suit%20against%20the,lis%20pendens%20against%20the%20property)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20homeowners%20subsequently%20posted%20a,with%20the%20notice%20of%20contest).
The contractor’s counsel claimed they weren’t aware of the bond and
therefore didn’t sue the surety in time. The Fourth DCA reversed the
trial court’s leniency and held that **strict compliance** with the
statute was required: the owners **followed the statutory scheme** by
recording the bond and contest and having the clerk serve the lienor at
the address on the lien, which is all the law
requires[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=those%20arguments).
Because the contractor *“did not file suit against the surety within
sixty days,”* the lien was *“extinguished automatically by operation of
law,”* and the bond had to be released as a matter of
law[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084).
The court explicitly rejected arguments about lack of notice to counsel
or due process, noting that the statute’s notice provisions (mailing to
the lienor’s address of record) were followed and must be strictly
construed[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=No%20one%20disputes%20the%20homeowners,We%20reject%20those%20arguments)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084).

**Case Illustration – Jon M. Hall Co. v. Canoe Creek:** This 2024 Second
DCA decision underscores the unforgiving nature of these deadlines in a
complex scenario. The contractor (Hall) recorded an initial lien
(\~$825k) and the owner bonded it off and filed a contest, then Hall
recorded an **amended lien** for a much higher amount (\~$1.8
million)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=Jon%20M,notice%20of%20contest%20of%20lien)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=Two%20weeks%20later%2C%20the%20property,the%20amended%20claim%20of%20lien).
The owner likewise bonded off the amended lien and filed a second notice
of contest *during* the ongoing
litigation[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=Two%20weeks%20later%2C%20the%20property,the%20amended%20claim%20of%20lien)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=Five%20months%20later%2C%20the%20property,as%20a%20matter%20of%20law).
Hall filed suit against the owner for the amended lien but **neglected
to join the surety** on the bond. After 60 days passed, the trial court
granted summary judgment for the owner, finding the lien
extinguished[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Following%20a%20hearing%20on%20May,and%20Hall%27s%20petition%20timely%20followed)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Thus%2C%20as%20illustrated%20by%20Hiller,22%282).
On appeal, Hall argued that because its original suit was timely, it
thought there was *“no time limit”* to add the surety for the amended
lien. The Second DCA disagreed: once the **Amended Claim of Lien** had
been transferred to a bond and contested during litigation, the plain
language of §§ 713.22(2) and 713.24(4) gave Hall **60 days** to bring an
action against the bond (i.e. add the
surety)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Thus%2C%20as%20illustrated%20by%20Hiller,22%282)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=,%E2%80%9D).
Hall’s failure to do so within the statutory window meant the lien was
automatically extinguished, despite the pending
lawsuit[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Thus%2C%20as%20illustrated%20by%20Hiller,22%282)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=The%20court%20then%20turned%20back,as%20a%20matter%20of%20law).
The court emphasized that lien deadlines are designed to be **“definite
and certain”** not just for the parties but for lenders and buyers
relying on clear
records[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=In%20giving%20its%20ruling%2C%20Florida%E2%80%99s,24%20Florida%20Statutes)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Our%20supreme%20court%20has%20specified,%E2%80%9D%20Id).
Allowing an “open-ended” extension by way of an amended lien or belated
joinder of the surety would undermine the certainty of the statutory
scheme[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=The%20court%20rejected%20the%20argument,08%284%29%28b%29%2C%20Florida%20Statutes%2C%20as%20follows).
In sum, **a lienor cannot ignore the shortened deadline on the theory
that an earlier filing (or original lien) tolled the time** – Florida
law *“cannot be bent”* to accommodate such
delays[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time).

**Takeaway:** Courts demand **strict compliance** with Chapter 713’s
time limits when a lien is transferred to a
bond[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time).
Filing a timely foreclosure lawsuit against the property owner is not
enough if the lien gets bonded off – the lienor must timely initiate an
action **against the bond (surety)** itself. Failing to sue the proper
party within the statutory period **extinguishes the lien rights
completely**, and neither the trial court’s equitable powers nor excuses
of ignorance will save the
lien[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time).
Judges have no discretion to extend these deadlines in lien cases,
unlike ordinary civil
cases[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time).
The consistent message in Florida precedent is that **“recovery may not
be allowed”** where the lienor does not strictly comply with the time
limits and notice requirements of the lien
law[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=seeking%20affirmative%20relief%20under%20the,4th%20DCA%201999).

## Transfers of Liens During Pending Litigation – One-Year Rule vs. 60 Days

A special scenario arises when a lien is **bonded off in the midst of an
ongoing lien foreclosure lawsuit** (as in Hiller, Rabil, and Jon M. Hall
above). Historically, lienors faced a trap: before 2005, § 713.24 lacked
clarity on time limits if the transfer happened after a suit was already
filed. Some owners exploited this by defending the lien suit until the
one-year period nearly ran, then **transferring the lien to a bond after
the year expired**, arguing that the lienor’s bond claim was
time-barred[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=lien%2C%20the%20clerk%20was%20required,legislature%20amended%20Florida%20Statute%2C%20Section).
Florida courts refused to allow such maneuvers, recognizing it would
unfairly let owners evade liability by a late bond
transfer[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=lien%2C%20the%20clerk%20was%20required,legislature%20amended%20Florida%20Statute%2C%20Section).
In **2005**, the legislature closed this loophole by amending
§ 713.24(4)[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=Consequently%2C%20in%202005%2C%20the%20Florida,by%20adding%20the%20following%20sentence).
The statute now provides that if a lien is transferred to security
**“during \[an enforcement\] proceeding,”** the lienor has **one year
from the transfer** to initiate an action against the bond (unless
shortened by a notice of
contest)[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CIf%20a%20proceeding%20to%20enforce,%E2%80%9D)[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=The%20appellate%20court%20reasoned%20that,one%20year%20of%20the%20transfer).
Moreover, if the lienor does sue the bond within that year, the law
treats it as relating back to the original lien action’s filing date,
ensuring continuity of
jurisdiction[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CIf%20a%20proceeding%20to%20enforce,%E2%80%9D).

Florida courts have enforced this **one-year-from-transfer rule**. For
example, in **The Cool Guys, LLC v. Jomar Properties, LLC** (Fla. 4th
DCA 2012), a contractor counterclaimed to foreclose a lien, the owner
bonded off the lien during the suit, and the contractor then waited
**two years** before filing a claim against the
bond[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=716084%20%28Fla,surety%20moved%20for%20summary%20judgment).
The trial court granted summary judgment to the owner, and the Fourth
DCA affirmed, finding *“no ambiguity”* in § 713.24(4)’s
language[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=The%20appellate%20court%20reasoned%20that,one%20year%20of%20the%20transfer).
Because the lien was transferred during pending litigation, the
contractor **“must bring a claim on the transfer bond within one year of
the transfer”**, and waiting two years was
fatal[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=The%20appellate%20court%20reasoned%20that,one%20year%20of%20the%20transfer).
The appellate court flatly rejected the argument that the statute only
applied to separate actions; even within the same case, the lienor had
to timely amend or sue on the
bond[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=until%20two%20years%20after%20such,of%20the%20Owner%20and%20Surety)[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=that%20if%20a%20lien%20foreclosure,one%20year%20of%20the%20transfer).
In essence, **the added sentence in § 713.24(4)** means exactly what it
says – if the lien gets bonded off during a lawsuit, the lienor must
assert rights against the bond within one year of that
event[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=The%20appellate%20court%20reasoned%20that,one%20year%20of%20the%20transfer)[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=This%20case%20demonstrates%20the%20clear,of%20the%20lien%20being%20transferred)
(unless the time is further shortened to 60 days by a contest, as
discussed).

**Practical implication:** When a lienor is already in court and the
owner “bonds off” the lien, the battlefront shifts to the bond. The
lienor should **immediately amend the complaint to add the surety (and
any new bond principal)** as defendants or file a new action on the
bond, *and must do so within one year at the very
latest*[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=This%20case%20demonstrates%20the%20clear,of%20the%20lien%20being%20transferred).
If the owner also files a Notice of Contest of Lien along with the bond
(a common tactic), the deadline truncates to **60 days** even in this
scenario[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Thus%2C%20as%20illustrated%20by%20Hiller,22%282)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=,%E2%80%9D).
As the Hall, Hiller, and Cool Guys cases illustrate, Florida courts will
not give lienors leeway simply because an original suit was timely filed
– the onus is on the lienor to **keep up with the procedural changes**
once a lien is transferred to a bond during litigation. In short,
**transferring a lien to a bond does not pause or extend the enforcement
period – it either keeps it at one year from transfer or shrinks it to
60 days if
contested**[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=,%E2%80%9D)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=Pursuant%20to%20the%20%E2%80%9Cunless%20otherwise,service%20of%20the%20notice%20of).
Lienors who fail to appreciate this will find their liens dissolved and
their bond claims lost. Owners, conversely, can confidently use the
transfer bond and notice of contest in tandem to expedite the resolution
or expiration of lien claims.

## Surety Liability and Bond Amount Limitations

A **lien discharge bond’s surety** promises to pay whatever sum the
lienor would have been entitled to from the property, up to the penal
amount of the bond. Florida Statutes § 713.24 sets the required bond
amount to ensure it covers the lien plus a cushion for interest, costs,
and attorneys’
fees[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=an%20amount%20equal%20to%20the,7).
Importantly, **Florida law permits recovery of reasonable attorneys’
fees by the prevailing party in a lien foreclosure** (including actions
on a transfer
bond)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=of%20the%20Florida%20Statutes3%20make,Sec).
Thus, one recurring issue is whether a surety can be liable for
attorneys’ fees exceeding the nominal bond amount.

Florida courts have held that a lien transfer bond **must cover the
lienor’s full judgment, including interest and any court-awarded fees,
up to the bond’s face
value**[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
Historically, older versions of § 713.24 capped the costs (including
fees) to a small sum (e.g. $100, later $500), and courts prior to 1987
enforced those caps
strictly[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=attorney%27s%20fees%20incurred%20by%20a,Inc)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=In%201987%2C%20the%20legislature%20amended,to%20limit%20an%20award%20of).
However, the legislature amended the statute in 1987 to delete the
explicit cap language and increase the cost allocation, signaling that
sureties should be exposed to *“all reasonable attorney’s fees”*
incurred by a lien
claimant[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=In%201987%2C%20the%20legislature%20amended,to%20limit%20an%20award%20of)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=liability%20for%20all%20reasonable%20attorney%27s,We%20disagree%20with%20Aetna%27s%20interpretation).
In **Aetna Cas. & Sur. Co. v. Buck** (Fla. 1992), the Florida Supreme
Court confirmed that under the amended § 713.24, an award of fees **is
not limited to $500** or any arbitrary
cap[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=liability%20for%20all%20reasonable%20attorney%27s,We%20disagree%20with%20Aetna%27s%20interpretation)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
The Court reasoned that the deletion of the old cap evinced an intent to
make the surety fully liable for reasonable fees as taxed by the
court[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=liability%20for%20all%20reasonable%20attorney%27s,We%20disagree%20with%20Aetna%27s%20interpretation).

Critically, however, the Supreme Court in *Buck* also held that the
statute **“does not permit the trial court to increase the liability of
the surety beyond the amount of the
bond.”**[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v)
In other words, while a prevailing lienor can obtain a judgment for the
entire amount due (including accrued interest and attorneys’ fees), the
surety’s obligation is capped at the bond’s penal sum unless additional
security is
posted[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
The court cited the rule that any part of a final judgment exceeding the
bond amount leaves the lienor as a general unsecured creditor for the
overage[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=in%20order%20to%20cover%20costs,is%20left%20with%20an%20unsecured).
This means if a lienor’s total recovery (lien principal, interest, and
fees) ends up higher than the original bond, the **surety pays up to the
bond limit** and no more – **unless** the bond has been increased
pursuant to
§ 713.24(3)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
For example, in *Buck*, the trial court had ordered the bond amount
increased post-judgment to cover added fees, and the surety challenged
having to pay more than the bond’s face
value[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=The%20trial%20court%20held%20that,court%27s%20final%20judgment%20and%20order)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=of%20the%20Florida%20Statutes3%20make,Sec).
The Supreme Court agreed with the surety on that point: the proper
course is to require **posting of an additional bond** if needed, rather
than simply making the surety pay beyond the original bond
amount[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
Thus, Florida law strikes a balance – **sureties are liable for the full
measure of a lienor’s damages, but only up to the security actually
provided**.

**Practical implications:** A lienor who prevails on a bonded-off lien
can recover fees and costs from the bond, but must be mindful of the
bond’s limits. If it appears the initial 25% (or $1,000) added for fees
will be insufficient (very common on smaller
liens[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=lien,cost%20the%20same%20to%20litigate)),
the lienor should timely invoke the procedure to **increase the bond**
(discussed below). Otherwise, any award above the bond amount may
require pursuing the bond principal (e.g. the owner or contractor who
procured the bond) directly for the shortfall, since the surety can
*discharge its obligation in full by paying the bond amount*. Owners and
sureties, on the other hand, should understand that **the bond is not a
hard cap on exposure if the court orders an increase** – failing to
increase a bond when warranted could leave the principal personally
liable for excess judgment amounts (and in some cases interest
continuing to accrue on the unpaid portion). In short, a valid lien
release bond guarantees payment to the extent of its penal sum, and
Florida precedent (from *Buck* and others) ensures lienors can recover
their entitled sums *within* that
security[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
But without proactive adjustments to the bond, the surety will not be on
the hook beyond the bond, even if the lienor’s actual losses are
greater[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).

## Increasing the Bond: Adequacy of Security and Common Issues

Section 713.24(3) allows a court to **increase the amount of a lien
transfer bond** upon motion by any interested party, if the initial bond
(or deposit) is **insufficient to cover the lienor’s attorneys’ fees and
court
costs**[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=transferred.,security%20to%20be%20warranted%2C%20the)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=require%20an%20increase%20in%20the,construction%20lien%20foreclosure%20and%20which).
This provision is crucial in practice because the statutory 25% margin
for fees is often too low for small lien
claims[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=lien,cost%20the%20same%20to%20litigate).
Several cases illustrate how courts handle requests to increase bond
security, and the pitfalls in enforcement:

  - **Evidence and Reasonableness:** Courts require competent evidence
    of **actually incurred** fees and costs related to enforcing the
    lien, and will not increase a bond based on speculative future
    fees[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=was%20addressed%20in%20Lake%20Eola,court%20denied%20the%20requested%20bond).
    For instance, a federal court in *Lake Eola Builders v. Metro. at
    Lake Eola* (M.D. Fla. 2006) denied a bond increase because the
    lienor failed to prove the amount of fees *already incurred* and
    directly tied to the lien foreclosure; anticipated future fees could
    not be
    included[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=was%20addressed%20in%20Lake%20Eola,court%20denied%20the%20requested%20bond).
    Similarly, in *Royal Marble, Inc. v. Innovative Flooring &
    Stonecrafters* (Fla. 2d DCA 2005), the trial court refused to
    increase a bond after finding the lienor’s evidence of fees was
    insufficient to establish
    reasonableness[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=was%20addressed%20in%20Lake%20Eola,court%20denied%20the%20requested%20bond)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=transfer%20bond%20is%20limited%20to,22).
    On appeal, the Second DCA dismissed the lienor’s certiorari
    petition, noting that **no irreparable harm** was shown – implying
    the issue could be addressed in a final judgment (i.e. the lienor
    might still recover fees from the bond up to its limit, and any
    excess could be sought from the defendant outside the
    bond)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=transfer%20bond%20is%20limited%20to,22).
    The upshot is that to successfully obtain a bond increase, a lienor
    must present solid proof (testimony, affidavits, expert opinion if
    needed) of the **amount of fees already accrued and their
    reasonableness** in the lien enforcement action.

  - **Enforcement of Increase Orders:** A more vexing problem is what
    happens if a court *does* order the bond amount increased, but the
    party who posted the bond (owner/contractor or surety) fails or
    refuses to provide the additional security. The statute does not
    explicitly prescribe a remedy in that
    event[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=%C2%A7713,its%20order%20increasing%20the%20bond)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=the%20home%20builder%E2%80%99s%20pleadings%2C%20which,16).
    In **Smith Original Homes, Inc. v. Carpet King, Inc.** (the *Carpet
    King* case, 2d DCA
    2005)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=This%20was%20precisely%20the%20issue,for%20attorneys%E2%80%99%20fees%20and%20costs)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=the%20home%20builder%E2%80%99s%20pleadings%2C%20which,16),
    a carpet supplier’s $7,000 lien was bonded off by a home builder.
    The lienor’s legal fees quickly exceeded the $1,700 provided for
    costs, so it moved to increase the bond by
    $28,000[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=of%20contract%20and%20sought%20to,15%7D%20The%20home).
    The circuit court agreed and ordered the builder to deposit the
    additional
    $28k[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=percent%20of%20the%20lien%20statutory,16).
    The builder, however, **failed to pay**, claiming it couldn’t afford
    the
    increase[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=the%20circuit%20court%20to%20which,16).
    The trial court then imposed sanctions – striking the builder’s
    pleadings – for failure to post the
    security[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=builder%20failed%20to%20pay%20the,16).
    On appeal, the Second DCA reversed, finding the court *abused its
    discretion*: **“There is no express authority for sanctions for
    failure to pay an ordered increase in a surety
    bond.”**[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=The%20home%20builder%20appealed,trial%20court%20lacked%20authority%20to)
    Although courts have inherent power to enforce orders, the appellate
    court noted the trial court made no finding of willful or bad-faith
    disregard (the builder’s nonpayment was based on claimed
    inability)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=The%20home%20builder%20appealed,trial%20court%20lacked%20authority%20to)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=effect%20of%20the%20reversal%20immunized,court%20was%20deferring%20to%20the).
    Absent evidence of willful contempt, severe sanctions like striking
    defenses were deemed improper – and indeed the statute itself is
    silent on penalties for
    non-compliance[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=The%20home%20builder%20appealed,trial%20court%20lacked%20authority%20to)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=no%20finding%20of%20willful%20disobedience,24).
    The *Carpet King* decision essentially left the lienor without the
    extra security and **“immunized the home builder from paying the
    statutorily-required additional security”** in that interlocutory
    stage[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=The%20home%20builder%20appealed,trial%20court%20lacked%20authority%20to)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=effect%20of%20the%20reversal%20immunized,court%20was%20deferring%20to%20the).
    The appellate court implied that it is up to the legislature to
    provide a clear remedy or sanction in such
    cases[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=no%20finding%20of%20willful%20disobedience,24).

From *Carpet King* and related commentary, a lienor in this situation
can attempt other measures: for example, move for an **order to show
cause** why the bond poster shouldn’t be held in contempt, giving the
court a chance to find willful disobedience if the party actually has
the ability to
pay[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=failure%20to%20increase%20the%20amount,24).
If willfulness is shown, a court could then strike pleadings or enter
default. But if the bond poster truly lacks funds (a “financial
inability” scenario), non-compliance is not willful contempt, and the
lienor may be *“left holding the bag”* – an order for more security that
cannot be
enforced[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=failure%20to%20increase%20the%20amount,24).
In practical terms, this means the lienor’s additional fee recovery is
unsecured unless the bond provider later becomes able to pay. This gap
in the statute’s enforcement mechanism has been criticized for
undermining the very protection the bond is supposed to afford “the
little guy” (small
subcontractors/suppliers)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=lien,cost%20the%20same%20to%20litigate)[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=effect%20of%20the%20reversal%20immunized,court%20was%20deferring%20to%20the).

**Practice Pointers:** When seeking a bond increase, lienors should: (1)
file the motion **well before** final judgment, as soon as it’s clear
fees will surpass the included 25%; (2) present detailed affidavits or
testimony to justify the amount; and (3) request the court set a
deadline for posting the increase. If the opposing party does not
comply, lienors should promptly pursue contempt proceedings and build a
record of that party’s ability to pay (e.g. evidence of assets or other
expenditures) to support a finding of willfulness if possible. On the
flip side, owners/contractors and sureties should **take court-ordered
increases seriously** – failure to post additional bond could lead to
contempt sanctions (if deemed willful) or, at the very least, leave the
owner personally exposed for attorney fee judgments beyond the bond.
Notably, the *Buck* decision confirms that **courts cannot simply make
the surety pay over the bond amount absent an increased
bond**[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
Therefore, an owner who cannot increase a bond might face the prospect
of a personal judgment for fees, while the surety’s liability would
remain capped at the original bond. It’s far better to avoid this
predicament by accurately estimating potential fees or promptly topping
up the bond when ordered.

## Procedural Requirements and Common Pitfalls

**Florida courts expect strict adherence to the procedural steps**
outlined in the lien transfer statute. Several recurring issues have
emerged in case law regarding compliance (or lack thereof) with these
procedures:

  - **Proper Execution and Recording of the Bond:** Section 713.24
    requires that the bond be issued by a **surety authorized to do
    business in Florida** and generally follow a statutory form
    (including identifying the lien, parties, and conditioned on payment
    of any sum found due). While published cases rarely focus on
    defective bond forms, a bond **not signed by the principal and
    surety, or not properly conditioned, could be deemed invalid**
    (making the transfer ineffective). In practice, clerks will usually
    refuse to accept an obviously nonconforming bond. To be safe,
    owners/contractors should use the Florida statutory bond form or a
    clerk-provided form to avoid any argument that the transfer was
    void. Once the bond (or cash deposit) is ready, it must be filed
    with the clerk, who will then issue a **Certificate of Transfer**
    and mail a copy to the lienor’s address as shown on the
    lien[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=Once%20the%20transfer%20has%20taken,insufficient%20to%20pay%20the%20lienor%E2%80%99s).
    **Failing to record the transfer or obtain the certificate means the
    lien is not officially transferred**, and the property may still be
    encumbered. This is a straightforward step, but it’s crucial.

  - **Serving Notice to the Lienor:** The statute mandates that the
    clerk send the transfer certificate to the lienor (at the address in
    the claim of lien). In *Rabil*, the contractor’s counsel complained
    they never received direct notice of the bond and contest, but the
    court noted the **clerk did mail notice to the address on the lien**
    (which happened to be the contractor’s office) as
    required[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20homeowners%20maintain%20that%2C%20when,See%20id)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=No%20one%20disputes%20the%20homeowners,We%20reject%20those%20arguments).
    That was sufficient service under § 713.22(2). The lesson: **Lienors
    must keep their mailing address on the recorded lien up to date and
    monitor any mail**. If a lienor moves or prefers notice to go to
    counsel, they should ensure the lien reflects that or otherwise
    track the public records. Once a Notice of Contest is recorded,
    **the 60-day clock starts upon service of that notice by the
    clerk**[law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/second-district-court-of-appeal/2024/23-1368.html#:~:text=to%20enforce%20the%20lien,1%29%20within%20which)
    – and service by mail to the lienor’s last known address is legally
    effective. A lienor who fails to receive or act on the notice will
    still be bound by the 60-day deadline (barring extremely unusual
    circumstances). There is **no requirement** that the owner or clerk
    serve the lienor’s attorney or provide a courtesy warning like “you
    must sue the surety” – the statute is deemed to put the lienor on
    notice of that
    obligation[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=No%20one%20disputes%20the%20homeowners,We%20reject%20those%20arguments).
    Accordingly, lienors should treat any transfer notification as
    urgent, and owners would be wise (though not required) to send an
    extra copy to the lienor’s attorney to preempt later arguments.

  - **Bringing the Correct Action (Joining the Surety):** A very common
    pitfall is lienors not **properly adjusting their lawsuit after a
    lien is transferred to bond**. Florida law is clear that once the
    lien is on a bond, any foreclosure action must be directed **against
    the bond and its surety** (and the principal who posted the bond)
    rather than the
    property[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=Pursuant%20to%20the%20%E2%80%9Cunless%20otherwise,service%20of%20the%20notice%20of)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=to%20commence%20an%20action%20on,60%20days%20after%20service%20of).
    In practical terms, that means if a lienor has already sued to
    foreclose the lien on the property, they need to **amend the
    complaint** to substitute a claim on the bond and add the surety as
    a defendant, or file a new action on the bond if no suit existed
    yet[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=Pursuant%20to%20the%20%E2%80%9Cunless%20otherwise,service%20of%20the%20notice%20of).
    Simply continuing the case against the owner without naming the
    surety is insufficient – the judgment must be against the bond
    security (else the lienor could end up with a judgment against an
    owner who may not be personally liable, especially if the owner was
    not in privity). The Hiller, Rabil, and Hall cases all involved
    lienors who **delayed adding the surety**, with disastrous results.
    For example, in *Hiller*, the lienor filed suit timely against the
    owner, but after the bond was posted and contested, the lienor still
    needed to sue the surety within 60 days. The lienor’s attempt to add
    the surety late was rejected and the bond
    released[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=to%20commence%20an%20action%20on,60%20days%20after%20service%20of).
    In *Hall*, the lienor filed suit on the amended lien but neglected
    to name the bond or surety until many months later – far beyond the
    60-day limit – so the lien claim was
    lost[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Thus%2C%20as%20illustrated%20by%20Hiller,22%282).
    The **correct approach** is: as soon as you learn of the bond
    transfer, **bring the surety into the lawsuit and assert your claim
    directly against the bond**. Florida law even allows the action on
    the bond to *relate back* to the original filing date if done within
    one year of
    transfer[cotneycl.com](https://www.cotneycl.com/lien-transfer-with-no-surety/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CIf%20a%20proceeding%20to%20enforce,%E2%80%9D),
    preserving priority, but only if you actually file it in time. If a
    lienor is unsure of the surety’s identity, they should obtain the
    recorded transfer documents from the clerk immediately (these will
    list the surety and bond number). Failing to include the surety
    means any judgment would not bind the surety, and after a year (or
    60 days if contested) the lien itself
    vanishes[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084).

  - **Amended Liens and Multiple Bonds:** Lienors sometimes record
    **amended liens** (to correct errors or adjust the amount) during
    the 90-day lien recording period. An amended lien is essentially a
    new lien claim. As seen in *Jon M. Hall Co. v. Canoe Creek*, if the
    lien amount increases, the owner has the right to **bond off the
    amended lien as
    well**[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=Two%20weeks%20later%2C%20the%20property,the%20amended%20claim%20of%20lien)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=%3E%20%5BThe%20contractor%E2%80%99s%5D%20argument%20,to%20take%20%E2%80%9Cadvantage%20of%20these).
    A mistake by Hall was assuming that the original bond covered the
    amended lien or that the original suit’s timing controlled – the
    court made clear that the **amended lien was a separate
    encumbrance** that had to be bonded off and contested, and that
    started a new 60-day clock for the **amended lien’s
    bond**[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=The%20court%20then%20turned%20back,as%20a%20matter%20of%20law)[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=%3E%20%5BThe%20contractor%E2%80%99s%5D%20argument%20,to%20take%20%E2%80%9Cadvantage%20of%20these).
    Owners and contractors should promptly bond off any *new or modified
    liens* to avoid leaving part of the claim on the property. Lienors
    must treat an amended lien as a new lien for enforcement timing: it
    does **not** relate back to an earlier lien for purposes of the
    owner’s right to contest or the deadline to sue on the
    bond[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=The%20court%20rejected%20the%20argument,08%284%29%28b%29%2C%20Florida%20Statutes%2C%20as%20follows).
    In short, each lien (original or amended) that is transferred to
    security must be enforced against that security within the
    applicable period or it will expire. If multiple liens from
    different lienors are filed, an owner can either post separate bonds
    for each lien or a blanket bond covering all (the statute doesn’t
    explicitly provide for a single blanket bond, so separate bonds are
    more common for clarity). Each lienor then has to pursue their own
    claim accordingly.

  - **Fraudulent or Invalid Lien Claims:** While not directly about the
    bond mechanism, it’s worth noting that if a lien itself is deemed
    **fraudulent or unenforceable**, that defense is equally good
    against the bond. For example, if a lienor failed to serve a
    **Notice to Owner** when required (as was the issue in *Aetna v.
    Buck*[library.law.fsu.edu](https://library.law.fsu.edu/Digital-Collections/flsupct/dockets/76925/76925ini.pdf#:~:text=BUCK%E2%80%98S%20lien%20is%20invalid%20since,owner%20requirement%20since%20he%20was)[library.law.fsu.edu](https://library.law.fsu.edu/Digital-Collections/flsupct/dockets/76925/76925ini.pdf#:~:text=this%20Court,to%20owner%20to%20perfect%20their)),
    the lien is invalid and the lienor cannot recover from the bond any
    more than they could from the property. In *Buck*, although the lien
    had been transferred to a surety bond, the Supreme Court focused on
    whether the lienor was in privity or had given notice to owner; they
    ultimately held the lien was unenforceable due to lack of notice
    (since the owner and contractor were distinct despite common
    ownership)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=We%20have%20for%20review%20Pappalardo,4%29%2C%20Florida%20Constitution)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=The%20trial%20court%20held%20that,court%27s%20final%20judgment%20and%20order).
    Thus, posting a bond doesn’t cure substantive defects in the lien
    claim – it only moves the security. Owners often assert the same
    defenses (defective lien, exaggeration, etc.) in the bond context. A
    bonded-off lien can also be challenged via a § 713.21 show-cause
    proceeding or on summary judgment, just like a regular lien. If
    declared fraudulent, the lienor can even lose the right to any
    recovery and face damages, causing the bond to be released. Sureties
    should be aware they can raise the owner’s defenses too, and if a
    lien is declared fraudulent or extinguished, the surety is off the
    hook.

In all these situations, **procedural precision** is vital. Chapter 713
is technical, and courts will not excuse non-compliance even if it leads
to harsh
results[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time).
For attorneys and parties, the best practice is to create **diary
deadlines** for any 60-day contest period and one-year periods,
double-check that **all necessary parties are in the lawsuit**, and
ensure that **paperwork is properly served and recorded** at each step.
The cases show that most “lien transfer bond” disasters are avoidable
with diligent adherence to the statute.

## Practical Recommendations for Stakeholders

  - **For Owners/Contractors (Posting a Discharge Bond):** To
    effectively “bond off” a lien, make sure to **calculate the bond
    amount correctly** (100% of lien + 3 years’ interest + 25% or
    $1,000)[paveselaw.com](https://paveselaw.com/five-options-for-responding-to-a-construction-lien/#:~:text=Option%201%3A%20The%20property%20owner,for%20costs%20and%20attorney%20fees)
    and use a reputable surety company. **Record the bond and obtain the
    clerk’s certificate** of transfer; verify the clerk sends notice to
    the lienor’s
    address[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20homeowners%20maintain%20that%2C%20when,See%20id).
    It’s wise to **immediately record a Notice of Contest of Lien**
    after bonding off – this will shorten the lienor’s window to 60
    days[paveselaw.com](https://paveselaw.com/five-options-for-responding-to-a-construction-lien/#:~:text=Option%202%3A%20%C2%A0The%20property%20owner,Claim%20of%20Lien%20is%20extinguished)
    and force them to act or lose the lien. Keep proof of
    mailing/service of the contest notice by the clerk in case of any
    dispute. If the lienor amends their lien or files additional liens,
    be prepared to **post additional bonds** or increase the existing
    bond to cover the new
    amount[hammerngavel.com](https://hammerngavel.com/blog/court-affirms-extinguishment-of-18-million-construction-lien-over-failure-to-timely-add-surety-to-lawsuit#:~:text=%3E%20%5BThe%20contractor%E2%80%99s%5D%20argument%20,to%20take%20%E2%80%9Cadvantage%20of%20these).
    And if a court orders an increase in the bond, **comply promptly** –
    not only to avoid potential sanctions, but to ensure the bond covers
    the full exposure (otherwise the owner’s personal assets might later
    be targeted for any shortfall). In summary, by diligently following
    the statute (and perhaps giving the lienor or their counsel a
    courtesy notice of the bond/contest), an owner or contractor can
    leverage § 713.24 to clear the property’s title and put significant
    **time pressure on the lienor** to prosecute their
    claim[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084).

  - **For Lienors (Contractors, Subcontractors, Suppliers):** The moment
    you receive notice (or learn) that your lien has been transferred to
    a bond, **act decisively**. First, ensure you have a copy of the
    transfer bond and any Notice of Contest – check the public records
    or clerk’s mailing. **Diary the 60-day deadline** from the contest
    recording (or the one-year deadline from bond recording if no
    contest). If you already have a foreclosure lawsuit pending, **amend
    your complaint immediately to add a count against the bond and name
    the surety as a
    defendant**[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=Pursuant%20to%20the%20%E2%80%9Cunless%20otherwise,service%20of%20the%20notice%20of).
    If no suit is filed yet, you must file a lawsuit on the bond before
    the deadline – suing the owner alone is not enough; explicitly sue
    for judgment against the bond/surety. Do *not* assume that because
    you sued the owner within one year, you can wait to add the surety
    later – you **cannot** under current
    law[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1731298.html#:~:text=to%20commence%20an%20action%20on,60%20days%20after%20service%20of)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=Thus%2C%20as%20illustrated%20by%20Hiller,22%282).
    Next, be meticulous with **statutory notices**: for example,
    continue serving the owner (now bond principal) with pleadings, and
    serve the surety via the Florida Department of Financial Services if
    required by Florida law. In preparing your case, **document your
    attorneys’ fees and costs** related to the lien foreclosure – if it
    becomes clear those will exceed the bond’s 25% cushion, file a
    § 713.24(3) motion to increase the bond **sooner rather than
    later**. Provide affidavits or testimony to substantiate the fees
    incurred[floridabar.org](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/practical-solutions-to-the-problems-resulting-from-the-real-life-application-of-floridas-construction-lien-transfer-bond-statute/#:~:text=was%20addressed%20in%20Lake%20Eola,court%20denied%20the%20requested%20bond).
    This not only protects your ability to recover fees but also signals
    to the other side the strength of your claim. Finally, be aware of
    your lien’s underlying validity: a transfer bond doesn’t fix a bad
    lien. If there was a mistake (like no Notice to Owner when
    required), consider whether you can still correct it or if you
    should pursue other remedies (e.g., a breach of contract claim
    without the lien). **Preserve all lien rights** by following every
    procedural step – Florida courts will not give leeway for “excusable
    neglect” in lien
    cases[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=No%20one%20disputes%20the%20homeowners,We%20reject%20those%20arguments)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time).
    Keeping a checklist for bonded-off liens – confirm bond recorded,
    contest served, suit filed against surety timely – can save your
    claim. In short, **treat a lien transfer bond as a new defendant and
    a ticking clock**: adjust your strategy to sue that bond quickly and
    accurately.

  - **For Sureties (Issuers of Lien Release Bonds):** Ensure that any
    bond you execute under § 713.24 meets the statutory requirements in
    form and amount, as an improperly executed bond could be challenged.
    Once the bond is in place, recognize that under Florida law you may
    be liable for the **full amount of any lien judgment (including
    interest and the claimant’s attorney fees)** up to the bond’s penal
    sum[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=liability%20for%20all%20reasonable%20attorney%27s,We%20disagree%20with%20Aetna%27s%20interpretation)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
    You cannot assume a $500 or small cap on fees – that was abolished
    in
    1987[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=liability%20for%20all%20reasonable%20attorney%27s,We%20disagree%20with%20Aetna%27s%20interpretation).
    Your liability, however, will not exceed the bond amount, so your
    risk is defined by the bond’s
    size[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/aetna-cas-and-sur-892947243#:~:text=court%27s%20interpretation%20that%20section%20713,v).
    Be alert to any motions to increase the bond: if the court orders
    additional security, coordinate with the bond principal to post the
    increase, or clarify whether a new bond is being filed, to avoid
    confusion about your total exposure. During litigation, it’s prudent
    for the surety (or its counsel) to **monitor the lien foreclosure
    proceedings** even before being formally joined, as an early
    heads-up can facilitate a smoother defense once you are named.
    Sureties can and should raise any defenses the bond principal had to
    the lien (e.g., payment, fraud, invalid lien) because if the lien is
    defeated, the surety owes nothing. Finally, keep in mind that if the
    lienor misses the enforcement deadline (60 days or one year as
    applicable) and the lien is extinguished, the surety’s obligation
    dissolves. In cases like *Rabil*, once the lien was automatically
    extinguished, the court directed the bond be released back to the
    principal/surety[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084).
    Thus, a surety has an interest in the owner utilizing the Notice of
    Contest to accelerate the deadline, since a lapse by the lienor
    means the surety is off the hook sooner. In sum, by writing the bond
    in the correct amount and staying vigilant in the ensuing
    litigation, sureties can fulfill their obligations and avoid
    surprises – their worst-case liability is paying a valid lien
    judgment (up to the bond limit), and their best case is that the
    lienor drops the ball and the bond is exonerated without payment.

**Conclusion:** Lien release bonds under § 713.24 are a powerful tool in
Florida construction disputes. Courts have developed a body of case law
that **strictly construes the statute’s technical requirements**: from
timely filing and notice to the proper parties, to limits on recovery
and bond increases. The overarching trend is one of **predictability and
balance** – owners and other bond posters can remove clouds on title and
hasten the resolution of lien claims, but they must fully comply with
the statute and provide adequate security; lienors can still secure
payment via the bond, but they must be meticulous and prompt in
enforcing their rights against that substitute security. The consistent
message in recent precedent (Hiller, Rabil, Hall, etc.) is that
**procedural missteps can be fatal to lien rights once a bond is in
play**. By understanding these decisions and heeding the practical
lessons, all stakeholders – owners, contractors, lienors, and sureties –
can navigate the lien transfer bond process with greater confidence and
avoid the pitfalls that have ensnared
others.[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/116041959.html#:~:text=To%20that%20end%2C%20%E2%80%9C,not%20comply%20with%20the%20time)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1872693.html#:~:text=The%20legislature%20has%20provided%20a,LLC%2C%2084)
