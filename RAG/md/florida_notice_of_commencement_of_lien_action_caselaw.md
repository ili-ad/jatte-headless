# Florida’s “Notice of Commencement of Lien Action” – Case Law, Compliance Errors, and Best Practices

## Background on §713.22(1) Requirements

Florida’s Construction Lien Law (Chapter 713) imposes strict deadlines
to enforce a recorded mechanic’s lien. Under **§713.22(1)**, a recorded
construction lien expires **after one year** unless the lienor *both*
files a foreclosure lawsuit within that year **and** records a notice of
the pending action (typically via a **lis
pendens**)[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2022/713.22#:~:text=,of%20lis%20pendens%20is%20recorded).
In effect, commencing the lien-foreclosure suit extends the lien beyond
the one-year period, but only as to those with notice – subsequent
purchasers or creditors without notice of the action are not bound by
the lien unless a lis pendens (often termed the *“Notice of Commencement
of Lien Action”*) is timely
recorded[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2022/713.22#:~:text=,of%20lis%20pendens%20is%20recorded)[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=Second%2C%20you%20want%20to%20record,%E2%80%9D).
Florida courts treat these timing and notice requirements as substantive
conditions of lien enforcement, not mere
technicalities[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1446303.html#:~:text=In%20Regal%20Wood%20Products%2C%20this,court%2C%20in%20affirming%20dismissal%2C%20said).
Failing to comply results in the lien’s automatic extinguishment by
operation of
law[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=Section%20713,pertinent%20part).

**Key Point:** The one-year deadline (or shorter if properly shortened
by the owner) is strictly enforced. A lienor must file suit within one
year of recording the claim of lien and should record a lis pendens at
the time of filing to preserve the lien’s effectiveness and priority
against third
parties[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2022/713.22#:~:text=,of%20lis%20pendens%20is%20recorded)[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=Second%2C%20you%20want%20to%20record,%E2%80%9D).

## Notable Florida Cases and Judicial Interpretations

### Strict Enforcement of the One-Year Limitation

Florida courts consistently hold that the one-year period in §713.22(1)
is to be strictly construed and not tolled or extended absent explicit
statutory
authority[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=filing%20had%20no%20such%20effect,Palmer%20at%20510)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1446303.html#:~:text=In%20Regal%20Wood%20Products%2C%20this,court%2C%20in%20affirming%20dismissal%2C%20said).
If a lien foreclosure action is not **commenced within one year** of
recording the claim of lien, the lien is automatically void and
unenforceable, regardless of equities. For example:

  - **Regal Wood Products, Inc. v. First Wisconsin Nat’l Bank (Fla. 4th
    DCA 1977):** The court affirmed dismissal of a lien foreclosure
    filed too late, emphasizing that the one-year limit “is not like an
    ordinary statute of limitation” but part of the lien right itself.
    If the year elapses without filing suit, the lien “becomes
    extinguished and is gone
    forever”[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1446303.html#:~:text=In%20Regal%20Wood%20Products%2C%20this,court%2C%20in%20affirming%20dismissal%2C%20said).
    In other words, the deadline is a *condition precedent* to
    maintaining a lien, not just an affirmative defense.

  - **Scott v. Haufler (Fla. 1st DCA 1988):** In a mortgage
    foreclosure’s surplus distribution, several mechanics’ lienors
    lost priority because they had not sued to enforce their liens
    within one year. Those liens were deemed discharged by §713.22(1)
    and §713.21(3) as a matter of
    law[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=discharged%20upon%20failure%20of%20those,We%20reverse)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=Section%20713,pertinent%20part).
    The court noted that lien priority normally relates back to the
    project’s Notice of Commencement, but “this assumes a valid and
    enforceable lien.” Liens not timely enforced are **not valid**, so
    they have no priority to
    assert[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=While%20it%20is%20true%20that,2d%2C%20Mechanics%20Liens%2C%20%C2%A7%2055).
    The result: the junior mortgage in that case took priority over all
    expired construction
    liens[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=foreclosure%20sale,We%20reverse)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=While%20it%20is%20true%20that,2d%2C%20Mechanics%20Liens%2C%20%C2%A7%2055).

  - **Foy v. Mangum (Fla. 5th DCA 1988):** A subcontractor filed an
    **amended** claim of lien after the owner’s interruption and
    recommencement of construction, and then sued within one year of the
    *amended* lien (but more than one year from the original lien). The
    trial court initially allowed foreclosure, but on rehearing
    reversed, and the appellate court affirmed the lien’s
    invalidation[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=a%20suit%20to%20foreclose%20his,We%20affirm).
    The key issue was whether filing an amended lien restarted the
    one-year clock. The court held it did **not** – §713.22(1)’s
    one-year runs from the original claim of lien date, absent explicit
    statutory
    extension[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=the%20Mangums%20on%20the%20ground,We%20affirm)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=The%20issue%20in%20this%20case%2C,filing%20in%20which).
    Allowing amendments to toll or restart the time “would defeat the
    purpose of the statute for a speedy conclusion” of lien
    claims[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=filing%20had%20no%20such%20effect,Palmer%20at%20510).
    This case underscores that lienors cannot circumvent the one-year
    limit by refiling or amending liens; only timely action in court
    preserves the lien.

### Shortened Enforcement Periods (60-Day Contest & Show Cause)

Florida law provides mechanisms for owners to accelerate the lien
enforcement deadline, and courts equally insist on strict compliance in
those scenarios:

  - **Notice of Contest of Lien (§713.22(2)):** When an owner records a
    **Notice of Contest of Lien**, the lienor’s time to file suit is
    shortened to **60 days** from service of the notice. If the lienor
    fails to sue within that 60-day window, the lien is automatically
    extinguished by statute on the 60th
    day[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=limitations%20in%20this%20case,from%20on%20other%20grounds%20by)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=v,without%20intervention%20of%20the%20court%E2%80%9D).
    In *Jack Stilson & Co. v. Caloosa Bayview Corp.* (Fla. 1973), the
    Florida Supreme Court held that filing an amended lien after a
    notice of contest **does not** toll or extend the 60-day
    deadline[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=to%20commence%20a%20lien%20foreclosure,suit).
    The lienor in that case attempted to refile its claim of lien to
    gain more time, but the court rejected this maneuver, reinforcing
    that the statutory deadline “means 60 days” – no exceptions. More
    recently, the Fourth DCA echoed this in *Pierson D. Construction,
    Inc. v. Yudell* (2003), stressing that a Notice of Contest operates
    *“by operation of law to discharge a lien on the sixtieth day,
    without intervention of the
    court.”*[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=v,without%20intervention%20of%20the%20court%E2%80%9D)
    A lienor who misses that 60-day deadline cannot revive the lien.

  - **Show Cause Summons (§713.21(4)):** Separately, an owner can file a
    motion in circuit court to **show cause** why a lien should not be
    enforced or extinguished. If the court issues a show-cause summons,
    the lienor must initiate foreclosure within 20 days (or another time
    set by the court) or the lien may be discharged. Florida courts
    treat these accelerated time frames with the same rigor – failure to
    timely respond by filing suit will result in the lien’s discharge
    *via* court order. For instance, in *Nachon Enterprises, Inc. v.
    Alexdex Corp.* (Fla. 3d DCA 1993), an owner filed a show-cause
    action even though the lienor had already filed a foreclosure in the
    proper court. The appellate court emphasized that because the lienor
    **had** in fact timely filed its action (albeit in county court,
    which was deemed a competent forum after a 1990 jurisdictional
    change), the lien could not be discharged under the show-cause
    procedure[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=to%20be%20strictly%20construed%2C%20as,01%284%29%20which%20specifically%20states%20that)[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=restricted%20by%20the%20State%20Constitution,or%20the%20laws%20of%20Florida).
    This illustrates that as long as the lienor *complies* with the
    shortened period (or has already sued), the lien remains safe; but
    had the lienor not done so, discharge would have been proper.

  - **Final Contractor’s Affidavit Timing:** When the lienor is a
    contractor in direct privity with the owner, §713.06(3)(d) requires
    a **Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit** be given to the owner at
    least 5 days before filing suit. Courts generally allow the
    affidavit to be given late (even after suit) so long as it’s before
    final judgment. However, if an owner’s contest or show-cause
    procedure shortens the enforcement period, even this prerequisite
    must be accomplished within that truncated window. In *Pierson D.
    Constr., Inc. v. Yudell*, the contractor filed the foreclosure
    complaint within a 60-day contest period but did not serve the final
    affidavit until after 60 days had run. The Fourth DCA held this was
    too late – although case law might excuse filing the affidavit
    **contemporaneously** with a timely suit, here the affidavit still
    needed to be delivered *before the 60-day deadline expired*. Filing
    it afterward could not revive the lienor’s
    rights[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=On%20December%205%2C%202000%2C%20the,court%20later%20dismissed%20the%20case)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=Pierson%20contends%20that%20its%20suit,2000%20filing%20of%20the%20lawsuit).
    This confirms that **all** statutory preconditions must be met
    within the applicable enforcement period.

### The Lis Pendens (“Notice of Commencement of Lien Action”) and Third-Party Interests

Recording a **lis pendens** in conjunction with a lien foreclosure
action is critical to preserve the lien’s effect against third parties.
The lis pendens serves as the public “Notice of Commencement of Lien
Action” contemplated by §713.22(1). Florida decisions highlight that
failing to record this notice (or recording it too late) can be fatal
when new interests arise:

  - **Decks N Such Marine, Inc. v. Daake (Fla. 1st DCA 2020):** A
    contractor filed a lien foreclosure lawsuit in 2006 but **waited
    seven years** (until 2013) to record a lis pendens in the public
    records[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=A%20recent%20case%2C%20Decks%20N,22).
    In the interim, the property owner executed a mortgage (recorded
    *before* the lis pendens). When the contractor later tried to
    foreclose that mortgage as a “junior lienholder,” the mortgagee
    moved for summary judgment, invoking §713.22(1)’s proviso that a
    continued lien “is not enforceable against creditors or subsequent
    purchasers…without notice, unless a notice of lis pendens is
    recorded”[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=Second%2C%20you%20want%20to%20record,%E2%80%9D)[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=construction%20lien%20foreclosure%20action%20in,a%20lienholder%20that%20could%20be).
    The First DCA agreed with the mortgagee: because the lis pendens was
    recorded after the mortgage, the mechanic’s lien could not trump or
    extinguish the mortgagee’s
    interest[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=construction%20lien%20foreclosure%20action%20in,a%20lienholder%20that%20could%20be).
    In essence, the belated lis pendens meant the mortgagee was a
    **protected third party without notice**, so the lien was
    ineffective against that mortgage. The contractor’s foreclosure
    could proceed against the owner’s interest *subject to* the
    mortgage, but the mortgage could not be foreclosed out. This case
    starkly illustrates that a lienor who files suit but neglects to
    promptly record a lis pendens risks losing **priority** to
    intervening encumbrancers. (Notably, the mortgagee in *Decks N Such*
    sought attorneys’ fees as a prevailing party under the Lien Law, but
    the court held that a junior lienor who merely prevails on priority
    is not entitled to fees under
    §713.29[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=is%20recorded,mortgagee%20prevailed%20on%20this%20issue).)

  - **Judicial View:** The courts treat the lis pendens requirement as
    integral to lien priority. A timely foreclosure action *without* a
    lis pendens still preserves the lien between the original parties
    (avoiding automatic expiration), but it offers no protection against
    third-party transferees who lack actual notice. Practically, if a
    bona fide purchaser or new lender records an interest after the
    one-year period has run (and no lis pendens of record), they take
    free of the
    lien[flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2022/713.22#:~:text=,of%20lis%20pendens%20is%20recorded).
    The lesson from *Decks N Such* and similar rulings is clear –
    **record the lis pendens at the outset** of the lawsuit. Ideally,
    the lis pendens should be recorded simultaneously with (or
    immediately after) the complaint, ensuring that anyone searching
    title will discover the pending lien action. Waiting even a short
    time can allow an intervening deed or mortgage to gain priority,
    nullifying the lien as to that interest.

  - **Content and Accuracy:** Although not a reported case, it bears
    noting that any **errors in the recorded lis pendens** (e.g.
    incorrect property description or case reference) could similarly
    undermine notice. The lis pendens must correctly identify the
    property and the action. An improperly indexed or defectively
    described notice might fail to impart constructive notice, leaving a
    subsequent purchaser “without notice” and thus outside the lien’s
    reach. Lienors should double-check that the lis pendens (the “Notice
    of Commencement of Lien Action”) is recorded in the correct county,
    refers to the proper parties/case, and matches the property’s legal
    description exactly.

### Statutory Compliance and Lien Validity

Florida lien law is highly technical, and courts require **meticulous
compliance** with statutory mandates to perfect and enforce a lien. Some
additional recurring compliance issues shown in the case law include:

  - **Proper Content of the Claim of Lien:** Section 713.08 specifies
    the required content for a claim of lien (e.g. names of owner and
    lienor, description of the work, property description, dates of
    work, service of Notices to Owner if applicable, etc.) and even
    provides a recommended
    form[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=WARNING%21)[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=CLAIM%20OF%20LIEN).
    It also requires a boldface warning to owners about the one-year
    enforcement period and their right to accelerate
    it[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=THIS%20LEGAL%20DOCUMENT%20REFLECTS%20THAT,OR%20TO%20DISCHARGE%20THIS%20LIEN).
    While minor errors or omissions that do not prejudice anyone may be
    excused by a
    court[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=However%2C%20the%20negligent%20inclusion%20or,defeat%20an%20otherwise%20valid%20lien),
    significant mistakes can invalidate the lien. For instance, a
    materially incorrect property description or failure to verify the
    lien could render it unenforceable. Likewise, omitting the required
    warning language about expiration might be deemed a prejudicial
    omission. In practice, lienors have lost lien rights due to filing
    liens with the wrong legal description or other fatal errors (though
    each case is fact-specific).

  - **Service of the Claim of Lien:** The lienor must serve a copy of
    the claim of lien on the owner within 15 days of recording
    (§713.08(4)(c)). Failure to serve in time makes the lien “voidable
    to the extent the delay is shown to be
    prejudicial”[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=provided%20for%20recording%20the%20original,claim%20of%20lien).
    A common mistake is neglecting this step or being unable to prove
    timely service, which an owner can exploit to challenge the lien’s
    validity.

  - **Filing in a Court of Competent Jurisdiction:** Prior to 1990, only
    circuit courts could hear equity cases like lien foreclosures, which
    occasionally led to confusion about where to file suit if the lien
    amount was small. Statutory changes now allow **county courts** to
    hear lien foreclosures within their monetary
    limit[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=Section%20713,01%284%29%20which%20specifically%20states%20that)[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=Pursuant%20to%20this%20Section%2C%20a,2d).
    In *Nachon Enterprises v. Alexdex Corp.*, a lienor correctly filed a
    \<$5,000 lien foreclosure in county court; the owner’s attempt to
    discharge the lien for filing in the “wrong” court failed, as the
    county court was indeed competent under the revised
    law[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=the%20lien%20is%20commenced%20in,01%284%29%20which%20specifically%20states%20that)[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=Court,2d%20314).
    The take-away is to ensure the lawsuit is filed in an appropriate
    court (county or circuit, depending on amount) so that it validly
    “commences an action… in a court of competent jurisdiction” as
    §713.22(1)
    requires[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=to%20be%20strictly%20construed%2C%20as,01%284%29%20which%20specifically%20states%20that)[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=restricted%20by%20the%20State%20Constitution,or%20the%20laws%20of%20Florida).
    An action filed in a court lacking jurisdiction would not count and
    thus wouldn’t extend the lien.

  - **Timing Nuances – Amendments and Parties:** As seen in *Foy v.
    Mangum*, an **amended lien** (even if allowed or required in some
    circumstances) does not reset the clock for
    enforcement[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=the%20Mangums%20on%20the%20ground,We%20affirm)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=The%20issue%20in%20this%20case%2C,filing%20in%20which).
    Similarly, adding new defendants or new claims after the one-year
    period can be problematic. Florida courts have held that if a
    necessary party (e.g. the property owner or a required interested
    party) is not joined within the statutory time, you generally cannot
    add them later once the time has
    run[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/harris-paint-co-v-890210524#:~:text=,May%203%2C%201977).
    All essential aspects of the foreclosure action should be in place
    within the limitation period.

**Judicial Trend:** Across decades of case law, Florida courts have
emphasized *speedy and certain resolution* of lien
claims[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/harris-paint-co-v-890210524#:~:text=,24%284%29%20is%20the%20sp).
The mechanic’s lien statute is an “abrogation of the common law” and
thus strictly
applied[casemine.com](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148684add7b049344d716a#:~:text=Florida%27s%20lien%20laws%2C%20Chapter%20713%2C,Effective%20October).
Courts show little leniency to lienors who miss deadlines or overlook
statutory steps, even if the result appears harsh. The rationale is to
balance the equities: while the lien law protects those who improve
property, it also seeks to provide clear end-dates and notice to owners
and third parties so that land titles are not clouded
indefinitely[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/harris-paint-co-v-890210524#:~:text=,24%284%29%20is%20the%20sp).
In short, *strict compliance* is the watchword: “Strict compliance is an
indispensable prerequisite for one seeking affirmative relief” under the
lien
law[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=filing%20had%20no%20such%20effect,Palmer%20at%20510).

## Common Lienor Pitfalls and Compliance Errors

Through these cases, several **recurring errors** by lien claimants
emerge:

  - **Missing the Enforcement Deadline:** Failing to file the
    foreclosure lawsuit within one year (or the shortened 60-day period
    if a contest was filed) is the most egregious and unfortunately
    common mistake. Once the statutory period lapses, the lien is
    extinguished
    automatically[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=None%20of%20the%20appellees%2Flienors%2C%20except,1),
    and no court action can revive it. Lienors sometimes miscalculate
    the deadline or delay hoping for payment, only to find their lien
    rights expired. **Example:** In *Scott v. Haufler*, multiple
    subcontractors let the one-year period run and their liens were
    deemed discharged, costing them any recovery from the foreclosure
    surplus[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=discharged%20upon%20failure%20of%20those,We%20reverse)[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=While%20it%20is%20true%20that,2d%2C%20Mechanics%20Liens%2C%20%C2%A7%2055).

  - **Failure to Record a Lis Pendens (Notice of Action):** As
    illustrated in *Decks N Such Marine*, not recording the **Notice of
    Commencement of Lien Action** (lis pendens) contemporaneously with
    the suit can allow third parties to intervene without notice and
    claim superior
    rights[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=construction%20lien%20foreclosure%20action%20in,a%20lienholder%20that%20could%20be).
    Lienors who overlook this step or record the lis pendens late risk
    their lien becoming unenforceable against subsequent purchasers or
    lenders. This is a critical error affecting lien **priority** – a
    timely lis pendens preserves the lien’s priority date, while a late
    or missing one can mean the lien will be junior to later-recorded
    interests (or void against them).

  - **Incorrect or Omitted Information in the Lien:** Mistakes in the
    **Claim of Lien** document itself – such as wrong property
    description, misstating the owner’s name, or failing to include
    required statutory warnings – can render a lien invalid. While minor
    typos may be forgiven if no one is
    misled[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=However%2C%20the%20negligent%20inclusion%20or,defeat%20an%20otherwise%20valid%20lien),
    a lien with a substantial error (e.g., listing the wrong lot or
    failing to describe the services and dates) might be deemed
    insufficient. For instance, a lien that doesn’t adequately identify
    the property can’t attach to that property. Lienors have also
    invalidated their liens by not signing or notarizing the claim
    properly, or by not attesting to the truth of the claim (a
    requirement since the claim must be sworn). Additionally, Florida
    requires that the claim of lien warn the owner of the expiration and
    possible contest – failing to provide this notice could be viewed as
    a statutory noncompliance that prejudices the owner by keeping them
    uninformed of their rights. In practice, always use the statutory
    form or a proven template to avoid content errors.

  - **Lack of Proper Service or Recording Procedures:** Even a correctly
    drafted lien can fail if not handled properly. Common procedural
    missteps include:
    
      - Not **serving the claim of lien** on the owner (or not being
        able to prove service) within 15 days, which can make the lien
        vulnerable to
        challenge[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=provided%20for%20recording%20the%20original,claim%20of%20lien).
    
      - Recording the lien in the wrong county or against an incorrect
        legal description, so that it doesn’t appear in the chain of
        title for the affected property.
    
      - Failing to record necessary documents like lis pendens, or
        failing to do required pre-suit notices (such as the Notice to
        Owner for those not in privity, or a Contractor’s Final
        Affidavit for those in privity). Each of these is a potential
        point of failure under Chapter 713.

  - **Assuming Extensions or Exceptions Apply:** Lienors sometimes
    believe they have more time or leeway than the statute provides –
    for example, assuming that a partial payment resets the clock (it
    doesn’t, though a new *final furnishing date* from additional work
    could extend the lien recording deadline, it does not extend the
    foreclosure deadline once the lien is recorded). Another false
    assumption is that an amended claim of lien or a later ancillary
    filing will “relate back” for purposes of the one-year limit.
    Florida courts have overwhelmingly rejected such
    arguments[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=to%20commence%20a%20lien%20foreclosure,suit)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1446303.html#:~:text=section%20713,23).
    With rare exception (such as a timely filed suit that is then
    amended to add a bond claim per *Scarfone v. Marin*, 1983, where
    relation-back was allowed because the action itself was commenced
    timely[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1446303.html#:~:text=We%20also%20reject%20Bergeron%27s%20assertion,lien%20to%20a%20surety%20bond)),
    if the key action wasn’t taken within the statutory period, the lien
    is void.

In summary, most lienor pitfalls boil down to **timing errors** and
**paperwork errors** – either missing a deadline or failing to comply
with the statute’s formal requirements.

## Practical Implications for Lienors: Maintaining Enforceability and Priority

Given the courts’ strict interpretation of §713.22(1) and related
provisions, lienors (contractors, subcontractors, suppliers) should
adopt meticulous practices to protect their lien rights:

  - **Diarize All Deadlines:** **Mark the one-year deadline** from your
    lien’s recording date on your calendar, and file the foreclosure
    lawsuit well **before** that date. If you receive a *Notice of
    Contest of Lien* from the owner, treat the 60-day deadline with
    urgency – it’s a hard
    cutoff[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=v,without%20intervention%20of%20the%20court%E2%80%9D).
    Similarly, if a court issues a show-cause summons, note the response
    deadline. Do not assume you can get an extension or that a court
    will excuse a late filing; courts will not. Starting the legal
    action early not only avoids last-minute mishaps but also can
    pressure the owner to resolve the claim.

  - **Record a Lis Pendens Immediately:** When you file the foreclosure
    action, **record a lis pendens** against the property at the same
    time. This is your “Notice of Commencement of Lien Action” to the
    world. By doing so, you preserve your lien’s priority against anyone
    who might acquire an interest after you’ve filed
    suit[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=Second%2C%20you%20want%20to%20record,%E2%80%9D)[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=construction%20lien%20foreclosure%20action%20in,a%20lienholder%20that%20could%20be).
    *Best practice:* prepare the Lis Pendens when preparing the
    complaint and submit them together – this way, there’s no gap during
    which a sneaky transfer or new mortgage could occur. Ensure the lis
    pendens accurately describes the property (matching your lien) and
    references the court case. Keep it recorded for the duration of the
    litigation (renew it if necessary per lis pendens rules) until the
    foreclosure is resolved.

  - **Use Statutory Forms and Checklists:** Given the technical
    requirements for a valid lien, use the statutory **Claim of Lien**
    form as a guide to avoid
    omissions[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=WARNING%21)[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=THIS%20LEGAL%20DOCUMENT%20REFLECTS%20THAT,OR%20TO%20DISCHARGE%20THIS%20LIEN).
    Include all required information: property description, names, work
    description, dates, amounts, service of notice to owner, etc. Don’t
    forget the warning about lien expiration and owner’s right to
    contest – it’s mandated by statute and alerts owners that the lien
    has a limited
    life[codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xl-real-and-personal-property/fl-st-sect-713-08/#:~:text=THIS%20LEGAL%20DOCUMENT%20REFLECTS%20THAT,OR%20TO%20DISCHARGE%20THIS%20LIEN).
    Before recording, double-check every detail (and have a colleague or
    attorney review if possible). Likewise, ensure you **serve** the
    claim of lien on the owner promptly and document that service (via
    certified mail, etc.) to prove compliance with §713.08(4). Small
    mistakes in paperwork can cause major problems later, so a careful
    upfront review is worth it.

  - **Fulfill Pre-Suit Conditions:** If you’re a contractor (in privity
    with owner), remember to provide the owner a **Final Payment
    Affidavit** at least 5 days before filing the foreclosure suit (or
    within the shortened period if facing a contest). Even though some
    courts have allowed late affidavits, it’s risky – noncompliance can
    lead to dismissal of your lien foreclosure (or, as in *Pierson*,
    dismissal with prejudice if the deadline
    passes)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=On%20December%205%2C%202000%2C%20the,court%20later%20dismissed%20the%20case)[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=Pierson%20contends%20that%20its%20suit,2000%20filing%20of%20the%20lawsuit).
    Similarly, lienors not in privity must have served a **Notice to
    Owner** at the project’s outset; a foreclosure action will fail if
    the Notice to Owner was never sent. In short, make sure all Chapter
    713 notice requirements that apply to your role (Notice to Owner,
    Contractor’s affidavit, etc.) are satisfied *before* you head to
    court.

  - **Respond to Owner’s Legal Actions Quickly:** If the property owner
    files a **Notice of Contest of Lien** (which you’ll typically
    receive by certified mail or the clerk’s mailing), immediately
    calculate 60 days out and treat that as your new lawsuit
    deadline[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1460985.html#:~:text=limitations%20in%20this%20case,from%20on%20other%20grounds%20by).
    If the owner files a motion to show cause in court, don’t ignore it
    – you’ll need to either already have a suit on file or file one
    within the time allowed by the court’s order. Lienors should also be
    cautious when an owner files bankruptcy or another action that might
    affect the lien; consult counsel in those cases because additional
    steps (like obtaining relief from stay) might be needed, but the
    state-law deadlines still matter.

  - **Maintain Priority – Foreclose All Necessary Parties:** When
    enforcing your lien, include all **junior interests** in the lawsuit
    (e.g., later mortgages, judgment creditors, or other lienors
    recorded after your lien) so that those interests can be foreclosed
    and eliminated if your lien
    prevails[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=First%2C%20you%20want%20to%20make,Decks%20N%20Sunch%20Marine%2C%20infra).
    However, be mindful that if your lis pendens was late or your suit
    was late, some of those interests might actually outrank you. The
    case law shows that a lien not enforced on time essentially loses
    its *relation-back*
    priority[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=While%20it%20is%20true%20that,2d%2C%20Mechanics%20Liens%2C%20%C2%A7%2055).
    Thus, to maintain your priority from the project’s start (the Notice
    of Commencement date), timely enforcement and notice are key. When
    in doubt about an intervening interest’s status, assume the burden
    is on you to prove they had notice of your lien action. The prudent
    approach is to act as though any delay could cost you priority.

  - **No “Do-Overs”:** Recognize that under Florida law, you typically
    **cannot refile or re-record** to fix a missed deadline. Once a lien
    is expired or a statutory window closes, the lien right is
    extinguished[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1446303.html#:~:text=In%20Regal%20Wood%20Products%2C%20this,court%2C%20in%20affirming%20dismissal%2C%20said).
    For example, filing a new claim of lien will not resurrect an old
    one, and an amended pleading after the limitations period won’t save
    a late
    claim[caselaw.findlaw.com](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1446303.html#:~:text=We%20also%20reject%20Bergeron%27s%20assertion,lien%20to%20a%20surety%20bond).
    Therefore, treat each step as if you only get one chance to do it
    right. If a deadline is approaching and you’re not ready (say, still
    negotiating payment), it’s usually safer to file the suit to
    preserve the lien and then consider staying or dismissing the case
    if payment is resolved, rather than letting the time run out. Owners
    and their lawyers are well aware of these deadlines and may simply
    “wait you out” knowing your lien will self-destruct.

By adhering to these practices, lienors can greatly increase the
likelihood their liens remain **enforceable and prioritized** in any
subsequent foreclosure or payoff. The overarching principle is to be
**proactive and precise**: meet every deadline early, obey every
statutory requirement to the letter, and give notice to all necessary
parties. Florida courts have made it clear that the onus is on the
lienor to comply with the statute if they wish to avail themselves of
the powerful remedy of a construction
lien[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foy-v-mangum-no-885480257#:~:text=filing%20had%20no%20such%20effect,Palmer%20at%20510).

## Conclusion

Florida’s courts have developed a consistent body of case law
underscoring that the *Notice of Commencement of Lien Action* (recorded
lis pendens) and the one-year enforcement period are crucial to the life
of a construction lien. The common theme is strict adherence to the
statute: lien rights are strictly time-bound and notice-dependent.
Missteps such as late filings, missing notices, or paperwork errors will
usually result in the loss of the lien – either outright extinguishment
or loss of priority against other interests. For practitioners and lien
claimants, the takeaways are clear. **Start early**, comply scrupulously
with all statutory requirements, and provide public notice of your lien
action to safeguard against intervening claims. By learning from the
recurring errors seen in Florida cases – missed deadlines, untimely lis
pendens, and noncompliant filings – lienors can avoid forfeiting their
rights and ensure that their liens remain a potent tool for getting
paid. In sum, Florida’s lien law rewards the diligent and punishes the
dilatory: those who promptly enforce and properly notify will preserve
their lien remedies, while those who do not will find their liens
**automatically dissolved by
law**[case-law.vlex.com](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/scott-v-haufler-no-892357309#:~:text=None%20of%20the%20appellees%2Flienors%2C%20except,1)
or subordinated to the rights of
others[floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com](https://www.floridaconstructionlegalupdates.com/foreclosing-junior-lienholder-and-recording-lis-pendens/#:~:text=construction%20lien%20foreclosure%20action%20in,a%20lienholder%20that%20could%20be).
Following the best practices outlined above will help construction
industry participants maintain lien enforceability and priority
consistent with Florida’s rigorous legal standards.
